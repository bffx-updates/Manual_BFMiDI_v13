TOPIC 1 - CONNECTING TO THE EDITOR
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Page: 1
Summary: Through the app, the controller's Wi-Fi, your home network or a USB cable — and even with
        no pedal at all, in offline mode.
Introduction: The editor is the same everywhere: on your phone, tablet and computer, inside the
        BFMiDi apps or in the browser. What changes is the path to the controller.
        The simplest way is to install the app for your device: it opens right away, looks for the
        pedal on its own and, if it can't find one, lets you edit anyway, on a copy stored on the
        device itself.

1.1. The BFMiDi apps
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Purpose: There's an official app for Android, iPhone/iPad, Mac and Windows. It's the same editor as
          in the browser, built in — so it opens instantly, in full screen, and updates through the
          store.
How to:
1. Android (7 or later): BFMiDi on Google Play.
2. iPhone, iPad (iOS/iPadOS 17+) and Mac (macOS 14+): BFMiDi Editor on the App Store.
3. Windows 10 and 11 (64-bit): download the installer from the downloads page.
4. Open the app. It shows the editor right away and, at the same time, looks for the controller on
   your network and on the controller's own network. Once it finds it, it connects by itself.
Fields and items:
- Opens before finding the pedal (behavior)
  Description: The app doesn't wait for the connection to show the editor: it opens in offline mode
             (a copy stored on the device) and switches to the real pedal as soon as it finds it.
             You'll see the switch in the connection indicator, the first button on the bottom bar.
- Home network first (behavior)
  Description: If the controller is on your home network (STA mode), the app prefers that path —
             your phone keeps its internet connection. Without a home network, it uses the
             controller's own Wi-Fi (AP mode).
             The app can also join the BFMIDI_WIFI network by itself when you ask, in the Conectar
             ao pedal (Connect to the pedal) window.
- USB cable in the desktop apps (Mac and Windows)
  Description: The Mac and Windows apps also connect over the USB cable and are the most comfortable
             way to update the controller and the USB Host. In the Android app and on iPhone/iPad,
             the connection is over Wi-Fi.
- App updates (maintenance)
  Description: Android, iPhone, iPad and Mac update through their own store. On Windows, download
             the new installer from the downloads page and install it over the old one — nothing is
             lost.
             The controller's firmware is a different thing: it's updated in SETTINGS › UPDATES.
Notes:
- Rather not install anything? The browser still works: see the next two cards.

1.2. The Conectar ao pedal (Connect to the pedal) window
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: CONECTAR AO PEDAL
Purpose: When the editor can't find the controller, this window opens by itself after a few seconds
          and offers the paths available on that device.
How to:
1. Turn the controller on and wait: while the radar spins, the editor is searching.
2. Choose a path: WI-FI (join the pedal's network), USB (over the cable — computer only) or OFFLINE
   (edit without a pedal).
3. Not sure? Tap Como conectar? (How to connect?), at the bottom of the window, to see the
   step-by-step.
Fields and items:
- The radar (status)
  Description: It spins while the editor looks for the controller, closes in green when it connects
             and turns red if the attempt fails. Once connected, the window closes by itself.
- WI-FI — Conectar à rede do pedal (join the pedal's network) (path)
  Description: In the apps, this card joins the BFMIDI_WIFI network by itself (the system asks for
             permission the first time). In the browser, it shows the network and password so you
             can join through your device's settings.
- USB — Conectar pelo cabo (connect over the cable) (path)
  Description: It only appears where USB port access exists: in Chrome or Edge on a computer and in
             the Mac and Windows apps. The browser asks you to authorize the controller's port.
- OFFLINE — Usar sem conexão (use without a connection) (path)
  Description: Closes the window and lets you edit a copy stored on the device. See the No pedal:
             offline mode card.
- Reopening the window (shortcut)
  Description: Closed it by accident? Tap the first button on the bottom bar (the connection
             indicator): it opens this window again.
Notes:
- During an update the window doesn't open, even if the connection drops — the drop is part of the
   flashing process.

1.3. Over the controller's Wi-Fi
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SETTINGS › WIFI
Purpose: The controller creates its own Wi-Fi network. It's the path that works anywhere, with no
          router and no internet.
How to:
1. Turn the controller on. The Wi-Fi comes up by itself and stays on for 90 seconds waiting for
   someone to connect.
2. On your phone, open Settings › Wi-Fi and join the BFMIDI_WIFI network. The password is
   bfmidi@editor — the same on every controller.
3. If your device warns that the network “has no internet”, that's expected: stay connected. The
   editor lives in the pedal.
4. Open the app, or open the browser at http://192.168.4.1 (or http://bfmidi.local).
Fields and items:
- BFMIDI_WIFI network, password bfmidi@editor (Wi-Fi)
  Description: The name and password are fixed and the same on every unit — they're printed here on
             purpose, so you never have to rely on reading the pedal's display.
             With two controllers turned on in the same place, both networks have the same name;
             turn one off while you set up the other, or put both on your home network.
- The addresses (browser)
  Description: http://192.168.4.1 always works on the pedal's network. http://bfmidi.local works on
             the pedal's network and on your home network.
             Type it with http:// in front: without it, Safari and Chrome run a Google search
             instead of opening the editor.
- Using your home network (optional)
  Description: In SETTINGS › WIFI, tap SCAN, choose the network, type the password and tap CONNECT.
             From then on the pedal can be reached at http://bfmidi.local from any device in the
             house, and you keep your internet while you edit.
             Only 2.4 GHz networks show up: it's a limitation of the chip.
- The Wi-Fi turned itself off (help)
  Description: With nobody connected, the radio turns off after 90 seconds. To bring it back: turn
             the controller off and on again, or use a footswitch combo set to WI-FI ON/OFF (in
             SETTINGS › BANKS). When you turn it on with the combo, the pedal's display shows the
             network, the password and the address for a few seconds.
- Mac: Local Network permission (help)
  Description: On macOS 15 or later, the browser needs Local Network permission (System Settings ›
             Privacy & Security › Local Network). Without it, the Mac joins the pedal's Wi-Fi but
             the page keeps loading forever. In the Mac app, the system asks for the same permission
             the first time: answer Allow.
Notes:
- The pedal's Wi-Fi password isn't your home network's password, and it never changes.

1.4. Over the USB cable (computer)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Purpose: On a computer you can talk to the controller over the cable, with no radio at all: it's the
          most stable path for making lots of changes and for updating.
How to:
1. Use the Mac or Windows app, or open the online editor in Chrome or Edge.
2. Plug the USB cable into the controller's DEVICE port.
3. In the Conectar ao pedal window, choose USB and authorize the controller's port. The first time,
   the editor reloads the page once — that's normal.
Fields and items:
- Where the cable isn't available (limitation)
  Description: The editor opened from the pedal itself (192.168.4.1 or bfmidi.local) doesn't offer
             USB — there you're already connected over Wi-Fi. Safari doesn't give access to the USB
             port either, and on phones and tablets the connection is always over Wi-Fi.
- The online editor (address)
  Description: The address https://bffx-updates.github.io/Editor_BFMiDi_v14/ works over the cable
             only: because it's a secure site (https), the browser won't let it talk to the pedal
             over the local Wi-Fi.
- When the cable is better (when to use)
  Description: Unstable home Wi-Fi, lots of changes at once, uploading many images and icons, and
             whenever you update an S2 controller or the USB Host.
Notes:
- When connected over the cable, the first button on the bottom bar shows USB in blue.

1.5. No pedal: offline mode
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: BOTTOM BAR — NO PEDAL
Purpose: You can open the editor and build presets away from the controller. The editor works on a
          copy stored on the device itself, which responds as if it were the pedal.
How to:
1. In the apps, offline mode is automatic: they open in it and switch to the pedal when they find
   it.
2. In the browser, choose OFFLINE in the Conectar ao pedal window.
3. To bring your work to the controller: BACKUP › BACK UP in offline mode, connect to the pedal and
   use RESTORE with the same file.
Fields and items:
- What gets stored (local copy)
  Description: The copy starts with the factory pack and keeps everything you edit: presets,
             settings and images. It stays on this device only — it doesn't go to the controller by
             itself.
- OFFLINE indicator (status)
  Description: The first button on the bottom bar shows the Wi-Fi icon crossed out in red. Tapping
             it opens the connection window.
- Resetting the copy (maintenance)
  Description: In SETTINGS › FACTORY RESET, the OFFLINE MODE card has the RESET LOCAL COPY button:
             it takes the copy back to the factory pack. The controller isn't touched.
Notes:
- Anything that depends on the hardware (tests, updates, USB devices, preset editors) is unavailable
   in offline mode, and the screen says why.

TOPIC 2 - GETTING STARTED
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Page: 1
Summary: The settings worth making once, right at the start, before you build your setlist.
Introduction: The controller leaves the factory ready to plug in and play, but five minutes of setup
        at the start save a lot of work later — especially telling it which controller model you
        have and which effects unit you use.

2.1. Five settings before you play
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Purpose: In order: check the model, tell it which gear you use, build your first preset, set up a
          footswitch in LIVE and choose your shortcuts.
How to:
1. Check the model in SETTINGS › HARDWARE. It must match what's written on your controller.
2. Tell it which gear you use in SETTINGS › FRIENDLY MODE: from then on, commands show up by name
   (“Delay”, “Reverb”) instead of numbers.
3. Build your first preset: the next topic shows you how.
4. Set up a footswitch in LIVE mode, for example to turn the drive on and off.
5. Choose your shortcuts in SETTINGS › EDITOR › SHORTCUTS: they appear as buttons on the MAIN card.
Fields and items:
- Why the model matters (attention)
  Description: The model tells the controller which pins exist: display, LEDs and footswitches. The
             wrong model leaves the display dark or footswitches unresponsive.
             On S3 boards, a blank unit starts up as BFMIDI-S3 8SW+: if yours is a NANO+, MICRO or
             6SW+, change the model before anything else.
- Saving is automatic (default)
  Description: The editor saves by itself less than a second after each change. The save button, on
             the right of the bottom bar, changes color to show the status. If you'd rather save
             manually, turn it off in SETTINGS › EDITOR.
- Try it without a pedal (tip)
  Description: The apps open in offline mode: you can get to know the editor and build presets even
             before turning the controller on.

TOPIC 3 - MY FIRST PRESET
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Page: 1
Summary: What a preset is, how to tell your effects unit which sound to load, and how to test it
        with your foot.
Introduction: Think of it this way: your effects unit already has lots of saved sounds; the BFMiDi
        is the remote control that, with a tap of your foot, makes it jump to the sound you want. A
        BFMiDi preset is one of those shortcuts.
        There are 10 banks (A to J) with 6 presets each — 60 shortcuts in total.

3.1. The four steps
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: PRESET SCREEN
Purpose: Choose the preset, say which sound it calls, give it a name and step on it. Saving is
          automatic.
How to:
1. On the console, on the left, tap the A1 readout to choose the bank and one of the keys 1 to 6 to
   choose the preset. The controller follows along.
2. On the MAIN card, choose the PC (the sound on your effects unit) and check the CHANNEL. If in
   doubt, channel 1.
3. Type the name in the name pill, right below the keys — it's what shows on the controller's
   display.
4. Step on the preset's footswitch: your effects unit jumps to the chosen sound.
Fields and items:
- PC — the effects unit's sound (selection)
  Description: PC stands for Program Change: it's the number of the sound on your effects unit. With
             Friendly Mode on, the list shows the name your effects unit uses (for example A01-1)
             instead of the number.
             Above 127, the BFMiDi also sends the Bank Select each device expects — you just choose
             the sound.
- CHANNEL (selection)
  Description: The MIDI channel is “which device listens”. Most come set to channel 1. With more
             than one device in the chain, each one goes on its own channel.
             OFF means the preset doesn't send any PC — useful when it's only there to set up LIVE
             mode.
- Preset name (text)
  Description: Up to 16 characters. It works as a label for the controller's display; your effects
             unit doesn't see this name. Usually it's the song's name.
- Stepped on it again and the bank changed? (attention)
  Description: By default, stepping again on the footswitch of the preset that's already playing
             moves up one bank (A1 → B1). That's RECLICK, configurable in SETTINGS › BANKS › PRESET
             CALL. And holding the active preset's footswitch enters LIVE mode.
- The sound didn't change? (help)
  Description: Check in this order: (1) the cable goes from the BFMiDi to the effects unit (MIDI OUT
             → MIDI IN); (2) the preset's channel is the one the effects unit listens to; (3) the PC
             exists on the effects unit.
             Still nothing? Use MIDI SEND in SETTINGS › TESTS: it sends a command right away and
             shows whether the effects unit responds.
Notes:
- The save button (the floppy disk, on the right of the bottom bar) turns green when everything is
   saved.
- Repeat for presets 2, 3, 4… and then for the other banks. A bank is usually a show; a preset, a
   song.

TOPIC 4 - HARDWARE
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Page: 1
Summary: Each model's physical connections: USB and MIDI ports, expression, external footswitches
        and power.

4.1. What plugs in where
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: PHYSICAL CONNECTIONS
Purpose: Choose your controller's family and model to see its panel and ports. The ports vary by
          version.
Fields and items:
- Controller model (selection)
  Description: Choose your BFMiDi's family and variant. The S3 family is the current line, with a
             new processor and more memory for images and icons: 8SW+, NANO+, MICRO and 6SW+.
             In the editor, the same selector is in SETTINGS › HARDWARE, and it only offers the
             models for the processor of the connected pedal.
- Top view (image)
  Description: Where the footswitches (1 to 6, LIVE and GLOBAL, when present), the display and the
             port labels are.
- Rear view (image)
  Description: The panel with that model's physical ports. Each port type is explained in the items
             below.
- USB DEVICE (port)
  Description: The main USB port. Plugged into a computer, the controller shows up as a USB MIDI
             device, opens the editor over the cable and receives updates. Plugged into the USB port
             of a Kemper Player, it's what makes the rig name, the tuner and the Kemper editor work.
             On S3 boards it's USB-B (the square connector).
- USB HOST (port)
  Description: The opposite of DEVICE: here you plug devices into the controller — USB effects units
             like the Valeton GP-5, the IK TONEX ONE and the Neural DSP Nano Cortex, MIDI keyboards,
             another BFMiDi.
             On the BFMIDI-3 and S3, it recognizes what was plugged in by itself and also includes
             Bluetooth. Setup in SETTINGS › HOST.
- MIDI TRS and MIDI DIN5 (port)
  Description: MIDI outputs for effects units and traditional gear: a mini jack (TRS) and the
             classic 5-pin connector. Both carry the same signal — use the cable or adapter that
             fits your device.
- EXP (expression pedal) (port)
  Description: On the “+” models. Input for an expression pedal (TRS): the movement becomes a
             continuous CC. Setup, calibration and output range in SETTINGS › FOOTSWITCHES.
- 2SW (two external footswitches) (port)
  Description: On the “+” models. Input for a dual pedal (two simple footswitches, no LEDs). Each
             button gets its own function in SETTINGS › FOOTSWITCHES, and can have an indicator on
             the display.
- 9V (power) (port)
  Description: A standard 9 V pedal power supply (center negative). The power supply is required to
             update the USB Host, because at that point the USB cable comes out of the DEVICE port.
Notes:
- Ports vary by model: USB HOST with Bluetooth on the BFMIDI-3 and S3; EXP and 2SW only on the “+”
   variants.

TOPIC 5 - THE EDITOR SCREEN
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Page: 2
Summary: The console where you pick the bank and preset, the MAIN card with what the preset sends,
        the pedal screen and the bottom bar.
Introduction: This is where you'll spend most of your time. On a computer it has three columns side
        by side; on a phone the same pieces are stacked one below the other, and the pedal screen
        sits behind the DISPLAY button.

5.1. Overview (computer and tablet)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: PRESET SCREEN — COMPUTER
Purpose: In a wide window — a computer, the Mac and Windows apps, a tablet in landscape — the editor
          shows three columns: the console, the middle card and the pedal screen.
Fields and items:
- Column 1 — the console (navigation)
  Description: The bank readout, the keys for the six presets, the preset name and the PRESET | LIVE
             | LAYER bar.
- The footswitch list (summary)
  Description: Computer only: one row per footswitch with the icon it shows on the screen, the mode,
             the MIDI command (with its friendly name) and the channel. The colored bar is the LED
             color.
             Tapping a row takes you straight to LIVE mode, with that footswitch open for editing.
- Column 2 — the middle card (editing)
  Description: In PRESET mode it's the MAIN card (what the preset sends, plus the shortcuts). In
             LIVE mode it becomes the card for the selected footswitch.
- Column 3 — the pedal screen (visual)
  Description: An accurate preview of what the controller will show, with the appearance settings
             right below it. When you open a shortcut from the MAIN card, its settings appear here,
             without leaving the screen.
- Bottom bar (navigation)
  Description: Connection, settings, home, copy/paste and save. Details in the card The bottom bar.
Notes:
- The screenshots in this manual come from the real editor screen in version 14.5. Tap a screenshot
   to see it at full size.

5.2. On a phone
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: PRESET SCREEN — PHONE
Purpose: On a phone the pieces are stacked: the console on top and the MAIN card below. The two
          round buttons at the top of the card switch between the settings and the pedal screen.
Fields and items:
- CONFIG and DISPLAY (round buttons)
  Description: CONFIG shows what the preset sends (PC, channel, extras) and the shortcuts. DISPLAY
             replaces the card's content with the preview of the pedal screen; the SETTINGS button
             below the preview opens the window with the name position, layout, font and colors.
- In LIVE mode (behavior)
  Description: The footswitch card has the same two round buttons: one for the mode settings, the
             other for how the icon looks on the screen.

5.3. The console: bank, preset, name and mode
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: CONSOLE
Purpose: The console is the controller's map: ten banks, six presets in each. It's how you move
          through your setlist and switch between PRESET and LIVE.
How to:
1. Tap the A1 · SET BANK readout to pick another bank.
2. Tap one of the 1 to 6 keys to pick the preset. The controller follows along.
3. Use the bar at the bottom of the console to switch between PRESET and LIVE.
Fields and items:
- The readout (bank)
  Description: Shows the active bank and preset (A1). Tapping it opens CHOOSE BANK, the grid with
             the ten letters. Banks turned off in SETTINGS › BANKS appear dimmed.
             In LIVE mode the readout becomes FX · SWITCH n and the keys pick the footswitch you're
             editing.
- Keys 1 to 6 (preset)
  Description: The lit key is the preset you're editing — and the one the controller is playing. On
             four-footswitch boards keys 5 and 6 stay dimmed: those presets exist in memory, but
             your foot can't reach them.
- The name pill (text)
  Description: This is the preset name field (up to 16 characters) — tap it and type.
- PRESET | LIVE (mode)
  Description: The most important switch on the screen. In PRESET you edit what the preset calls up;
             in LIVE, what each footswitch does inside it. The switch really changes the
             controller's mode, and the pedal changes screens along with it.
- SINGLE LAYER / DUAL LAYER (layer)
  Description: In PRESET mode, it turns the second layer of LIVE functions on or off for this preset
             (the six footswitches get a second page). In LIVE mode, the same button becomes LAYER 1
             / LAYER 2 and picks which layer you're editing.

5.4. Choosing the bank
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: CHOOSE BANK
Purpose: The grid with the ten letters opens when you tap the readout. One tap on a letter takes you
          straight to that bank, without stepping through the ones in between.
Fields and items:
- Dimmed letters (banks turned off)
  Description: These are the banks turned off in SETTINGS › BANKS › ACTIVE BANKS. Turning off the
             ones you don't use makes footswitch navigation skip right over them.
- Banks and setlist (tip)
  Description: A bank is usually a show, a set or a band; the six presets inside it, the songs.

5.5. The MAIN card
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: MAIN CARD
Purpose: What the preset sends when it's called up — PC, channel and extra messages — and the row of
          shortcuts to the rest of the editor.
Fields and items:
- PC (selection)
  Description: The sound your multi-effects unit loads when the preset is called up. It goes up to
             600: above 127 the BFMiDi also sends Bank Select in the format of the device chosen in
             Friendly Mode.
- CHANNEL (selection)
  Description: The MIDI channel of the device that receives the PC. The device name appears next to
             the number when the channel is set up in Friendly Mode. OFF = the preset doesn't send a
             PC.
- EXTRAS (button)
  Description: Each tap adds an extra message that goes out together with the PC (it starts as a PC
             on channel 1). Once there is at least one extra, the list shows up below CHANNEL. See
             the next card.
- STAGE MODE (shortcut)
  Description: Always the first in the row: opens the full-screen stage view. It has its own topic
             in this manual.
- Device editors (automatic shortcuts)
  Description: When a Valeton GP-5, an IK TONEX ONE or a Neural DSP Nano Cortex is plugged into the
             USB Host — or when Friendly Mode is set to KEMPER PLAYER — a button in the device's
             color appears that opens its preset editor. These buttons don't take the place of your
             shortcuts.
- Your shortcuts (shortcuts)
  Description: Buttons for the settings screens you use most. You choose which ones appear in
             SETTINGS › EDITOR › SHORTCUTS. On a computer the shortcut opens the settings in the
             third column, next to the preset; on a phone it takes you to the settings screen.
Notes:
- Shortcuts only appear in PRESET mode. In LIVE mode the middle card is the footswitch card.

5.6. Extra messages
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: EXTRA MESSAGES OPEN
Purpose: Besides the main PC, the preset can send up to 12 more messages when it's called up —
          that's how you set up the whole scene in one tap.
How to:
1. Tap EXTRAS, next to CHANNEL: each tap adds a line, which starts as a PC on channel 1.
2. On each line, the round button next to the number switches between PC and CC.
3. Choose the number, value (for a CC) and channel of each line.
Fields and items:
- Up to 12 messages (list)
  Description: Each row is a PC or a CC, with its own channel. Change the sound on your
             multi-effects unit, turn on the delay, set the volume of another device and change the
             mixer scene — all in the same preset.
- EXP value — the expression pedal in this preset (special value)
  Description: On boards with an expression input, the value list of a CC row ends with EXP (PEDAL
             DE EXPRESSÃO) (expression pedal). That row doesn't send MIDI: it tells the expression
             pedal where to point while this preset is active.
             The preset's target replaces the CC and channel from the expression card (which become
             just the default) and works even with that card turned off. You can have more than one
             EXP row, so the same pedal controls two targets.
- The sending order (detail)
  Description: When the preset is called up, the PC and the extras go out first; then the initial
             state of the LIVE footswitches; and only then the LED and the screen. Slow devices that
             miss the first messages can get a pause in SETTINGS › HARDWARE › SEND TIMING.

5.7. The pedal screen and the preset's look
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: PEDAL SCREEN — PRESET
Purpose: See exactly what the controller will show before you step on anything — when calling up the
          preset and when playing in LIVE — and adjust the name and colors.
How to:
1. Use the PRESET and LIVE tabs at the top of the preview to see both screens.
2. Tap the preview (or POSITION) to move the name and choose the layout.
3. Adjust the font, style and colors in the blocks below the preview: the preview updates as you go.
Fields and items:
- The preview (visual)
  Description: Draws what the controller shows, with the background, name and icons. Footswitches
             whose function has no MIDI channel don't appear — just like on the pedal screen.
- PRESET — preset name (text)
  Description: FONT SIZE (− / +) and STYLE (B = bold) of the name on the screen. EXT. SWITCHES
             decides whether the external footswitch indicators (ESW1 and ESW2) appear in this
             preset.
- APPEARANCE (name colors)
  Description: The name colors: NAME (the text), OUTLINE and TAG (the bank/preset label).
- SCREEN COLORS (backgrounds)
  Description: The two full-screen backgrounds: one for the PRESET screen, the other for the LIVE
             screen. Each can be a color, a gradient or an image you uploaded in SETTINGS › IMAGES.

5.8. Name position and per-preset layout
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: NAME POSITION · PRESET
Purpose: Drag the name anywhere you like on the screen and choose how the icons are arranged — just
          for this preset, if you want.
How to:
1. Drag the name in the preview. The position shows below as a percentage (X and Y).
2. Choose the screen layout: GLOBAL follows the unit's default; the others apply only to this
   preset.
3. Tap APPLY. Nothing changes until you do.
Fields and items:
- The layouts (selection)
  Description: GLOBAL inherits the layout from SETTINGS › DISPLAY. L1 to L4 are fixed icon
             arrangements and Custom lets you drag each icon and choose its size. The PRESET screen
             also has Nenhum (None — just the big name) and Lista (List — the presets in sequence).
             Dragging an icon in an L1–L4 layout turns the screen into Custom.
- Why per preset (use)
  Description: A song that needs four big effects can have its own layout while the others use the
             list. If you don't change anything here, the preset keeps following the global layout.
- Center (shortcut)
  Description: Moves the name back to the center of the screen.

5.9. The bottom bar
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: BOTTOM BAR
Purpose: Five buttons that work across the whole editor: connection, settings, home, copy/paste and
          save.
Fields and items:
- Connection (status)
  Description: The color tells you how you're connected: green STA (home network), yellow AP (the
             pedal's Wi-Fi), blue USB (cable) and OFFLINE with the Wi-Fi icon crossed out in red.

- Settings (navigation)
  Description: The gear opens the menu with the fifteen settings destinations, from FRIENDLY MODE to
             FACTORY RESET.
- Home (navigation)
  Description: The orange pill takes you back to the preset screen, wherever you are.
- Copy and paste (actions)
  Description: The sliders button opens the menu to copy and paste a preset, a layer or a bank —
             and, in LIVE mode, the settings from one footswitch to another.
- Save (status and action)
  Description: With auto save on (the default), the editor saves on its own less than a second after
             each change, and the floppy disk changes color: red = there's a pending change, orange
             = saving, green = everything saved. With auto save turned off, tap it to save.

5.10. Copy and paste

2. Go to the destination and open the same menu: now it offers PASTE.

  Description: Preset takes everything it sends plus the LIVE footswitches. Layer takes only the six

  Description: In LIVE mode the menu copies and pastes a footswitch's settings — mode, commands and

TOPIC 6 - LIVE MODE
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Page: 3
Summary: Inside each preset, the footswitches become effect pedals. This is where the controller
        stops being just a sound selector.
Introduction: The controller works in two modes. In PRESET mode, stepping on a footswitch calls up a
        song. In LIVE mode, you're already inside a song and each footswitch controls something in
        it: turns on the drive, taps the delay time, raises the volume for the solo.
        LIVE mode is set up per preset: the same footswitches do different things in each song. And
        each preset can also have two layers (L1 and L2) — twelve functions per song on a
        six-footswitch board.

6.1. How to set up a footswitch
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: LIVE MODE SCREEN — COMPUTER
Purpose: Switch to LIVE, pick the footswitch and tell it what to do. Always in that order.
How to:
1. On the console bar, tap LIVE. (On a computer, clicking a footswitch in the list also takes you to
   LIVE.)
2. Use keys 1 to 6 to pick the footswitch. The readout changes to FX · SWITCH n.
3. At the top of the middle card, tap the mode name and choose one of the ten modes.
4. Fill in the mode's fields. In the third column (on a phone, the round DISPLAY button), set the
   icon and the screen colors.
Fields and items:
- Choosing the footswitch (selection)
  Description: In LIVE, the console keys stop being presets and become this preset's footswitches.
             The lit key is the one you're setting up.
- L1 and L2 — the two layers (layers)
  Description: Turn on the second layer with the SINGLE LAYER / DUAL LAYER button on the console bar
             (in PRESET mode). In LIVE, the same button picks whether you're editing LAYER 1 or
             LAYER 2.
             On stage, you switch layers with the LIVE button on boards that have one, or with a
             footswitch set to the » TO LAYER command.
- Entering and leaving LIVE with your foot (on stage)
  Description: Out of the box, holding the footswitch of the preset that's playing enters LIVE
             (SETTINGS › BANKS › PRESET CALL › LONG CLICK).
             Boards with a LIVE MODE button: one tap enters (and switches the layer), holding exits.
             Boards without that button: use a two-footswitch combo with the SW_LIVE action — and,
             inside LIVE, holding the combo for half a second goes straight back to PRESET mode. You
             can also set a footswitch to the » OUT LIVE MODE command.
- No channel, no icon (pedal screen)
  Description: A footswitch whose function has no MIDI channel doesn't show up on the controller's
             screen (or in the editor preview). That's why, with the factory pack — footswitches in
             STOMP with the channel OFF — the LIVE screen starts out empty: it fills in as you give
             the footswitches a channel.
             The exceptions are FAVORITE, which navigates instead of sending MIDI, and MUTE, which
             never shows up.
Notes:
- Setting up LIVE is optional: a preset that just calls up the sound is already useful and ready to
   play.

6.2. The ten operating modes
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: LIVE MODE · SWITCH 1
Purpose: Each footswitch runs one mode. The mode decides what happens when you step on it — and
          changes all the other fields.
How to:
1. Tap the mode name at the top of the footswitch card to open this list.
Fields and items:
- Which mode to choose (summary)
  Description: STOMP — turns an effect on and off. What most people use.
             SPIN — cycles through three values.
             RAMP — sweeps smoothly from one value to another.
             MOMENT (momentary) — works only while your foot is on it.
             MACROS — several commands in one press.
             TAP TEMPO — tap the tempo with your foot.
             SINGLE — one fixed command per gesture.
             STEPS — four-value sequencer in a loop.
             CONTROL — A/B selector between two targets.
             MUTE — the footswitch has no function.
- Does changing modes erase what I had? (detail)
  Description: No. Each mode's parameters are stored separately: if you try SPIN and go back to
             STOMP, STOMP is just as you left it.
Notes:
- STEPS and CONTROL are for the preset's footswitches. SW GLOBAL and the external footswitches use
   the other eight modes.

6.3. STOMP — on and off
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SW — STOMP MODE
Purpose: The classic mode: your foot turns an effect on and off, like a real pedal. A single STOMP
          can have up to three sections, each on a different foot gesture.
How to:
1. On the SHORT CLICK tab, choose the effect's CC and CHANNEL.
2. Want more functions on the same footswitch? Also set up LONG CLICK and RECLICK.
3. Set the LED color and the behavior in the options strip below.
Fields and items:
- SHORT CLICK / LONG CLICK / RECLICK (sections)
  Description: Three functions on the same footswitch: quick press, held press and double press. The
             behavior adapts to what you fill in: only the first section = classic stomp; two or
             three sections = two or three effects on the same footswitch.
             With RECLICK filled in, the short click waits a moment (about a third of a second) to
             see whether a second press is coming.
- CC or PC (round button)
  Description: The round button next to the number switches the message type: CC (the usual choice
             for turning effects on and off) or PC (to call up a sound). With Friendly Mode, the
             list shows the name of each command.
- CUSTOM — your own values (round button)
  Description: Normally, on sends 127 and off sends 0. The controls button next to CHANNEL opens
             CUSTOM VALUES so you can pick other values — see the next card.
- FAVORITE (round button)
  Description: The star turns the section into a shortcut: instead of sending MIDI, stepping on it
             takes you to another bank and preset, landing in PRESET or LIVE mode (and, in LIVE, on
             layer 1 or 2). Use it to “jump to the next song” without leaving LIVE.
- Options (strip)
  Description: LED — the ring color for this footswitch.
             FIRES — sends the command as soon as the preset is called, without waiting for you to
             step on it.
             STARTS — the effect starts on or off.
             INVERT — the LED lights when off instead of when on.
             The LONG CLICK and RECLICK tabs also have BACK: when turned off, the screen goes back
             to showing the short-click icon.
- SINGLE RING (switch)
  Description: With two or three sections, the LED ring is split — one color per section. Turning on
             SINGLE RING (only on the SHORT CLICK tab) lights all three LEDs together, in the color
             of the last section you triggered.
Notes:
- When in doubt, start with just SHORT CLICK. You can add the other sections later without redoing
   anything.

6.4. Custom values
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: CUSTOM VALUES
Purpose: For devices that don't use 127 for on and 0 for off. Each value has a field, a percentage
          and a slider — all three change the same number.
How to:
1. Tap the controls button next to CHANNEL.
2. Set VALUE OFF (top) and VALUE ON (bottom) by typing, dragging the slider or dragging the number
   itself.
Fields and items:
- When to use it (use)
  Description: Devices that turn on at 64, parameters you want at 30% and 80%, or a volume that only
             goes as high as the solo needs. The same custom values exist in MACROS, MOMENTARY,
             SPIN, RAMP, TAP TEMPO and SINGLE.

6.5. TAP TEMPO — tap the tempo with your foot
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SW — TAP TEMPO MODE
Purpose: You tap the song's tempo with your foot and the controller sends it to the delay — or to up
          to three targets.
How to:
1. Choose your device's tap CC and the channel. ADD TAP adds up to three targets.
2. For each one, choose MODE 1 (sends CC + 127) or MODE 2 (CC + 127 followed by CC + 0) — it depends
   on what your device expects.
3. Tap the tempo with your foot in time with the song. The LED blinks at the tapped tempo.
Fields and items:
- LONG PRESS (gesture)
  Description: Holding the footswitch can send another command, with its own CC, channel and values
             — for example, turning on the tuner. With no channel on LONG PRESS, holding doesn't
             change the icon on the screen.
- BPM on the screen (visual)
  Description: When you tap the tempo, the controller shows the BPM on a temporary card. How long it
             stays up, and whether it shows the average or the last two taps, is set in SETTINGS ›
             DISPLAY. Turning the card off doesn't erase the tempo.
- What else uses the beat (integration)
  Description: STEPS in SYNC TAP follows the tapped tempo, and STAGE MODE shows the BPM on the top
             bar. With a Valeton GP-5 plugged into the USB Host and set up in Friendly Mode, a CC
             119 target sends the delay time in milliseconds straight to it.

6.6. SINGLE — one command per gesture
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SW — SINGLE MODE
Purpose: Each gesture always sends the same thing. No on/off — it's a pure command button, with up
          to four messages per gesture.
How to:
1. Choose the gesture tab: SHORT CLICK, LONG CLICK or RECLICK.
2. Fill in up to four slots, each one CC or PC, with a value and a channel.
Fields and items:
- When to use it (use)
  Description: For commands that don't have two states: calling up a scene, firing a sample,
             advancing the sheet music, clearing a looper. And for the controller's own navigation
             commands (changing bank or preset without leaving LIVE), which are at the end of the CC
             list, marked with ».
- SINGLEs work as a group (LED and screen)
  Description: Only the last SINGLE you stepped on stays lit, like radio buttons. On the screen, the
             ones that weren't the last use the INACTIVE colors.

6.7. MOMENTARY — only while your foot is down
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SW — MOMENTARY MODE
Purpose: On when you press, off when you let go: it's a doorbell button, not a switch. Up to four
          messages per press.
How to:
1. Fill in up to four slots with CC and channel; use custom values if the device doesn't use 127/0.
2. If you like, adjust the LED behavior in LATCH and LED fade.
Fields and items:
- What it's for (use)
  Description: Effects you want for just one passage: a kill switch, a freeze, a two-note boost.
             Lift your foot and it's over.
- LATCH and STARTS (LED)
  Description: LATCH keeps the LED lit until the next press, simulating an on/off — useful when the
             device toggles by itself on each pulse. With latch on, STARTS decides whether the LED
             is already lit when the preset is called (just the LED, no MIDI).
- LED fade (LED)
  Description: FADE ON and FADE OFF (in milliseconds) make the LED light up and go dark slowly; the
             animation can be TOGETHER (all three LEDs at once) or BAR (filling up in sequence).
             Only the LED: MIDI and the screen change instantly.

6.8. MACROS — several commands in one press
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SW — MACROS MODE
Purpose: One footswitch sends several messages at once — and sends the off values when you step on
          it again.
How to:
1. Fill in up to four slots with the commands that should go out together, each with its own ON and
   OFF value.
2. If you like, use the three gesture sections, just like in STOMP.
Fields and items:
- The typical case (example)
  Description: The “big refrain pedal”: one press turns on the drive, raises the delay, cuts the
             chorus effect and turns up the volume — four messages, a single footswitch. Turn it off
             and everything goes back to how it was.
- Differences from STOMP (detail)
  Description: MACROS has SINGLE RING and BACK, like STOMP, but no FAVORITE.

6.9. SPIN — cycle through three values
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SW — SPIN MODE
Purpose: Each press moves on to the next of three values, in a cycle. The LED ring shows which one
          you're on.
How to:
1. Choose the CC and channel and set the three values. Up to three slots send together on each
   press.
2. If you like, use LONG PRESS to send a different command when you hold the footswitch down.
Fields and items:
- The three states (cycle)
  Description: Each press cycles 1 → 2 → 3 → 1. Great for three gain levels, three delay times,
             three positions of a parameter.
- 3 COLORS (LED)
  Description: Normally the ring lights one LED per position, all in the same color. With 3 COLORS
             on, all three LEDs light together and each position has its own color — easier to read
             from a distance.
- When the preset is called (detail)
  Description: With firing turned on, SPIN starts at position 1 and sends the first value right
             away. With it off, the LED blinks while it waits for the first press.

6.10. RAMP — sweep smoothly
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SW — RAMP MODE
Purpose: Instead of jumping from one value to another, the controller travels the whole way — as if
          you were turning the knob by hand.
How to:
1. Set the range (minimum and maximum) and the RISE and FALL times.
2. Choose the CURVE and the BEHAVIOR.
Fields and items:
- TOGGLE, HOLD and LOOP (behavior)
  Description: TOGGLE: each press reverses the direction. HOLD: rises while your foot is down and
             comes back when you let go. LOOP: goes back and forth by itself, nonstop.
- The curve (selection)
  Description: LINEAR at constant speed, EXP slow at the start, LOG fast at the start, SINE smooth
             at both ends. It makes a real difference on volume and filter.
Notes:
- RAMP only works inside LIVE mode — it doesn't fire when the preset is called.

6.11. STEPS — CC sequencer
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SW — STEPS MODE
Purpose: When it's on, the footswitch steps through four values on the same CC, in a loop, by itself
          — as if you were turning a knob in time with the song.
How to:
1. In SEQUENCE, choose the CC and channel and fill in the four values.
2. Choose the timing: MANUAL (a time in milliseconds per step) or SYNC TAP (a note value per step,
   based on the beat).
3. If you like, fill in TURN EFFECT ON: a second CC that gets ON when the sequence starts and OFF
   when it stops.
4. Choose how the footswitch triggers it: ON/OFF or MOMENTARY.
Fields and items:
- The four values (sequence)
  Description: The sequence goes through the values in order, in a loop. When you turn it on, the
             first step goes out right away; when you turn it off, it stops immediately. Turning it
             back on starts over from the first one.
- MANUAL × SYNC TAP (timing)
  Description: In MANUAL, each step has its own time (up to 2 s) — so you can make irregular
             rhythms.
             In SYNC TAP, each step gets a note value based on the beat. The beat can come from Tap
             (tap a new tempo and the sequence follows) or from a Fixed BPM (20 to 300) — with a
             fixed BPM this footswitch ignores the tap, so you can keep a fixed rhythm here while
             another footswitch taps the delay tempo.
- ON/OFF × MOMENTARY (triggering)
  Description: ON/OFF: one press turns it on, another turns it off, and the sequence keeps running
             with your foot off. MOMENTARY: runs only while the footswitch is held down; let go and
             it stops and sends the effect's OFF.
Notes:
- Like RAMP, STEPS only runs in LIVE mode (or in HYBRID). Changing presets also stops the sequence.
- The LED blinks in time with the sequence.

6.12. CONTROL — A/B selector
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SW — CONTROL MODE
Purpose: One click alternates between two targets: the one coming in gets 127, the one going out
          gets 0. They're never both on or both off.
How to:
1. On the FX 1 tab, choose the CC, the channel and the LED color for the first target.
2. On the FX 2 tab, do the same for the second one.
3. In STARTS ON, choose which of the two is active when the preset is called.
Fields and items:
- CONTROL or a two-section STOMP? (concept)
  Description: Use CONTROL when the two effects are alternatives: two amps, two channels, two cabs.
             Use a two-section STOMP when they're independent and can be on at the same time.
- Colors and the screen (visual)
  Description: Each target has its own LED color, plus its own icon and name color on the screen.
             Since there's no “off”, the ring never goes dark — it changes color.
Notes:
- The values are fixed (127 and 0). For your own values, use STOMP.
- The 0 for the outgoing target goes first: that way the two are never on at the same time, not even
   for an instant.

6.13. MUTE — footswitch with no function
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SW — MUTE MODE
Purpose: The mode for when you want nothing on that footswitch: it sends no MIDI, lights no LED and
          doesn't show up on the screen.
Fields and items:
- Why it exists (use)
  Description: It's the way to disable a footswitch that was in use without erasing the rest of the
             preset — and the mode for a footswitch you don't want to use yet.

6.14. How each footswitch looks
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: FOOTSWITCH APPEARANCE
Purpose: Icon, label and colors of the little tile that represents the footswitch on the
          controller's screen — on and off.
How to:
1. On a computer, use the third column; on a phone, the round DISPLAY button on the footswitch card.
2. Tap PREVIEW ON or PREVIEW OFF to choose the icon (or the TEXT option).
3. Adjust BACK and BORDER (each with OFF and ON), the label and the name color.
Fields and items:
- Icon and label (content)
  Description: The icon comes from a built-in library (pedals, effects, symbols) or from the images
             you uploaded in SETTINGS › IMAGES. The label is the short text — “DRIVE”, “DLY”, “SOLO”
             —, up to 8 characters (6 per state in SPIN, SINGLE and CONTROL).
             Color icons (pedal photos) show as they are; line icons take the color you choose.
- FONT SIZE (selection)
  Description: AUTO lets the controller choose based on the available space; or set it to 9, 12, 18
             or 24 pt. If the text doesn't fit, the controller steps down one size at a time until
             it does. The size applies to the whole footswitch, not to each state.
- OFF and ON colors (colors)
  Description: Background, border and name color, each with one value for off and another for on.
             Well chosen, they make the effect's state readable from a distance.
Notes:
- In modes with tabs (STOMP and MACROS sections, CONTROL's FX 1/FX 2, SPIN positions, SINGLE
   gestures), the tab you pick on the mode card is the same one here — each section has its own icon
   and colors.

TOPIC 7 - STAGE MODE
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Page: 4
Summary: A full-screen landscape view with big lettering, so you can leave your phone or tablet on
        the stand during the show: the preset that's playing, the footswitches and the plugged-in
        device.
Introduction: STAGE MODE turns the editor into a panel for the show. There are two screens: STAGE 1
        shows the BFMiDi — the preset name in huge letters, the background image and the
        footswitches, which you can turn on and off with your finger — and STAGE 2 shows the device
        plugged into the controller — the effect chain and the knobs of the GP-5, the TONEX ONE or
        the Nano Cortex, editable live.
        The stage settings (colors, font, images) stay only on the device where you opened it: none
        of it goes to the controller.

7.1. Opening Stage Mode and STAGE 1
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: STAGE 1
Purpose: STAGE 1 is the general-purpose panel: the BFMiDi preset in big letters, the preset image
          and the footswitch icons — the same ones shown on the controller's screen.
How to:
1. On the PRESET screen, tap STAGE MODE — it's always the first button in the shortcut row of the
   MAIN card.
2. The stage always opens on STAGE 1. On a phone it fills the whole screen in landscape.
3. Tap a footswitch icon to turn the effect on or off; tap a preset in the bottom row to switch
   songs.
Fields and items:
- The marquee (preset name)
  Description: The preset name at the largest size that fits, on up to two lines. Above it, the bank
             and preset (A1) and whether the controller is in PRESET MODE or LIVE MODE; below it,
             the Friendly Mode device.
- The image (background)
  Description: By default it's the same background as the controller's screen for that preset (the
             PRESET one or the LIVE one, depending on the mode). In the apps you can also use the
             app gallery or one of your own photos — see Stage images.
- The footswitches (icons)
  Description: The same icons as on the pedal screen, with the number and a small bar in the LED
             color. Footswitches with no MIDI channel stay blank, just like on the controller.
             Tapping the icon does the same as a short click with your foot: it turns the effect on
             or off and sends the MIDI. This works for the on/off modes — STOMP, MACROS, CONTROL and
             STEPS (the last one only in LIVE); in the other modes you'll see the message “This mode
             has no on/off”.
- Bank presets (row)
  Description: The six presets of the current bank, with number and name. Tapping one calls that
             preset on the controller, exactly like stepping on its footswitch.
- The top handle (hidden bar)
  Description: The control bar stays hidden so it doesn't take up space. Tap the handle at the top —
             or any empty area of the screen — to show it. It hides again on its own after a few
             seconds.
Notes:
- Turning footswitches on and off from the stage requires an up-to-date controller. With older
   firmware the icon shows “Update the controller to use this”.
- With no pedal connected, the stage shows the OFFLINE tag; the connection item in the top bar
   becomes a button that opens the connection window.

7.2. The control bar
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: STAGE 1 — BAR OPEN
Purpose: Exit, switch between STAGE 1 and STAGE 2, see the preset, open the settings and keep an eye
          on the connection and the BPM.
Fields and items:
- EXIT (button)
  Description: Closes the stage and goes back to the editor. The ESC key on the keyboard does the
             same.
- STAGE 1 / STAGE 2 (button)
  Description: A single button that toggles between the two screens; the two little dots tell you
             which one you're on.
- PRESET A1 (info)
  Description: The bank and preset currently playing on the controller.
- SETTINGS (button)
  Description: Opens the stage settings, in four tabs. See the following cards.
- Connection, BPM and clock (status)
  Description: How you're connected (STA, AP or USB), the BPM of the last TAP TEMPO tapped on the
             BFMiDi and, if you turn it on, the clock. With no pedal, the connection turns into the
             OFFLINE button.
- On STAGE 2 (extras)
  Description: You'll also see the device selector (GP-5, TONEX, NANO CORTEX, KEMPER) and the EDITOR
             button, which opens the full preset editor on top of the stage.

7.3. STAGE 2 — the plugged-in device
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

3. Or: SETTINGS › HOST → the device's card → EDIT PRESET. In Stage Mode, the EDITOR button on STAGE

Fields and items:
- The device buttons (automatic shortcuts)
  Description: They appear before your own shortcuts, in each brand's color, and don't push any of

8.2. Valeton GP-5
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: PRESET EDITOR — VALETON GP-5
Purpose: The ten blocks of the GP-5 patch — noise gate, pre, drive, amp, cab, EQ, modulation, delay,

1. Tap a block in the chain (the list on the left on a computer; the row at the top on a phone) to

2. Turn the block on or off with the switch to the right of its name; tap the model name to change
   it.
3. Adjust the parameters with the slider or by typing the value.
4. When it sounds right, tap SAVE TO PEDAL. To go back to what was stored, tap DISCARD.
Fields and items:
- Patch volume (parameter)
  Description: The preset's overall volume, at the top, from 0 to 100.
- The blocks (chain)
  Description: Each block has its category color, the model highlighted and the parameters grouped

  Description: Up to 10 characters, as on the GP-5. A longer name gets cut when saving — the editor

- SAVE TO PEDAL and DISCARD (actions)
  Description: The GP-5 only stores with SAVE TO PEDAL; the editor confirms with “Preset saved to
             the pedal.”. Changing presets before that loses the edit. DISCARD reloads the stored
             preset.
             Closing with an unsaved edit asks what to do: save, discard, CLOSE WITHOUT SAVING (the
             edit keeps playing until the next preset change) or keep editing.

- With the USB Host's Transmit-only mode on (SETTINGS › HOST), the GP-5 editor is unavailable: in

  Description: For each footswitch and each gesture, one action. SHORT CLICK and LONG CLICK work on
             any footswitch. RECLICK means stepping again on the footswitch of the preset that's
             already playing (only on footswitches whose short click is CALL PRESET).
             Factory defaults: short = CALL PRESET, long = ENTER LIVE MODE and reclick = BANK UP.
- The actions (list)
  Description: CALL PRESET, STANDBY (shows the destination on screen without changing the sound, and
             you confirm afterward), BANK UP/DOWN (also in a standby version), PRESET UP/DOWN, ENTER
             LIVE MODE (only on the active preset's footswitch) and NONE. On long click and reclick
             there's also FAVORITE: go straight to a fixed bank and preset, arriving in PRESET or
             LIVE.
             At least one footswitch must call a preset (CALL PRESET or STANDBY) — otherwise there'd
             be no way left to change presets with your foot.
- STANDBY — browse without changing the sound (action)
  Description: With STANDBY you move through banks and presets while the song plays, seeing on
             screen where you're headed, and the sound only changes when you confirm. It's like
             finding the next song in the middle of the current one, without anyone hearing it.
- FOOTSWITCH COMBOS (pairs)
  Description: Stepping on two footswitches together triggers an action. There are ten pairs — 1+2,
             1+3, 1+4, 2+3, 2+4, 2+5, 3+4, 3+6, 4+5 and 5+6 — and each one gets an action:
             navigation, STANDBY, WI-FI ON/OFF or SW_LIVE (enters and exits LIVE).
             Combos work in PRESET mode; SW_LIVE keeps working inside LIVE. And inside LIVE, holding
             the SW_LIVE pair for half a second goes straight back to PRESET mode — even with layer
             2 on.
- ACTIVE BANKS (selection)
  Description: Turn off the letters you don't use: navigation then skips over them. At least one has
             to stay on.
Notes:
- A footswitch that's part of an active combo gets a slightly slower long click — that's the slack
   that lets you land the pair with both feet.
- Combos come empty from the factory: WI-FI ON/OFF, for example, has to be set up here.

9.8. DISPLAY — layouts, names and BPM
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SETTINGS › DISPLAY
Purpose: The default look of the controller's screen: what shows up in each mode, how the icons are
          arranged and the BPM card.
Fields and items:
- GIG VIEW (selection)
  Description: DEFAULT (both screens), ONLY PRESET or ONLY LIVE. Plus the switches that show or hide
             the preset name in LIVE and in BANK.
- LIVE MODE and PRESET MODE LAYOUT (layout)
  Description: L1 to L4 are fixed arrangements and CUSTOM lets you drag each icon and choose its
             size (up to the full screen height). PRESET also has NONE (just the big name) and LIST
             (the presets in sequence). These are the global values: a preset can have its own
             layout.
- Current only (LIVE CUSTOM) (switch)
  Description: With the CUSTOM layout in LIVE, Current only shows a single icon: the one for the
             last footswitch you stepped on, in a shared position and size. The Visible icons list
             still decides which ones can appear.
- Icon shape and bank names (style)
  Description: DEFAULT, CIRCLE or OCTAGON icons, set separately for each screen. Show bank preset
             names replaces the icons on the PRESET screen with the song names, with adjustable
             colors and size.
- BPM ON DISPLAY (group)
  Description: How long the BPM card stays on screen when you tap the tempo (OFF, 2, 5 or 10 s) and
             whether the number is the ABSOLUTE (last two taps) or the AVERAGE (average of the
             sequence). OFF only hides the card; the tempo still applies.

9.9. LEDS — brightness and ring colors
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SETTINGS › LEDS
Purpose: The rings around the footswitches are what you read out of the corner of your eye on stage.
Fields and items:
- LED Brightness (slider)
  Description: Applies to all the rings. A dark stage calls for low brightness; daylight, high.
- BANKS & PRESETS (selection)
  Description: What the color means in PRESET mode. BY LETTER: each bank has its own color. BY
             SWITCH: each footswitch has its own.
- LED Preview Live Mode (switch)
  Description: Gives the footswitches that are off a faint glow (adjustable) instead of leaving them
             dark, so you can see where your effects are in the dark.
- DEDICATED LEDS (colors)
  Description: The colors that signal LIVE MODE and LAYER 2. Pick very different colors: they warn
             you that every footswitch has changed function.

9.10. IMAGES — your own backgrounds and icons
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SETTINGS › IMAGES
Purpose: Upload your own background images and icons to the controller's screen.
How to:
1. Tap BANK BACKGROUND (full-screen images) or SW ICONS (the footswitch artwork).
2. Tap the + and choose a file. Adjust the crop and save.
3. Then pick the image in SCREEN COLORS (preset background) or in a footswitch's icon.
Fields and items:
- Shared memory (bar)
  Description: Images and icons share the same memory: about 2.4 MB on S3 boards and about 490 KB on
             earlier ones. The bar shows how much is in use — and deleting an icon frees up room for
             a background, and vice versa.
             Up to 40 images and 60 icons fit; each image can be up to 50 KB and each icon up to 30
             KB.
- The image editor (tool)
  Description: Before saving, you crop, zoom, adjust brightness and contrast, and can write text on
             top. It's worth darkening backgrounds a little: an image that's too bright hurts the
             readability of the preset name.
- In batches (actions)
  Description: Batch upload sends several images at once; Select lets you delete several. Over the
             USB cable, uploading is faster and more stable than over Wi-Fi.

9.11. HARDWARE — model, send timing and memory
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SETTINGS › HARDWARE
Purpose: Tell the controller which board it is, adjust the pace of MIDI sending and keep an eye on
          memory.
Fields and items:
- MODEL (selection)
  Description: First the family (1, 2, 3 or 3 S3), then the variant (NANO, MICRO, 6SW+, 7S, 8SW+…).
             It's not decoration: it's what tells the controller which pins exist. The editor only
             offers the models for the connected pedal's processor. Changing the model restarts the
             controller.
- SEND TIMING (pauses)
  Description: For devices that miss messages arriving too fast. Pause between HEADER and LIVE: a
             wait between the preset's PC/extras and the initial state of the footswitches.
             Per-channel pause: the minimum interval between two messages to one channel (a Bank
             Select + PC counts as one).
             Use the smallest value that fixes it: during the pause the controller waits, and an
             expression pedal on that channel becomes “steppy”.
- STORAGE (status)
  Description: How much memory is in use: Presets, Images and icons (a single bar, with the
             breakdown below) and Globals.
- Special cards (per model)
  Description: On MICRO boards, REMAPPING rotates the screen and rearranges the four footswitches.
             On the BFMIDI-1 7S, INVERT SCREEN rotates the screen 180°. Both restart the controller.

9.12. WIFI — network and connection
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SETTINGS › WIFI
Purpose: See how you're connected and, if you like, put the controller on your home network.
How to:
1. While connected through the pedal's network (AP) or the cable, tap SCAN.
2. Choose your network, type the password and tap CONNECT. The attempt happens without freezing the
   pedal.
Fields and items:
- CONNECTION STATE (status)
  Description: Four squares — OFF, AP, STA and USB — with yours highlighted. On your home network,
             the card shows the network and the address.
- The pedal's network (fixed)
  Description: BFMIDI_WIFI, password bfmidi@editor, address http://192.168.4.1 or
             http://bfmidi.local. It's the same on every unit.
- SCAN and CONNECT (actions)
  Description: SCAN only works through the pedal's network or the cable — on your home network it
             would drop its own connection. Only 2.4 GHz networks show up; hidden networks can be
             typed in by hand. The password is only stored after it actually connects: if it fails,
             the previous setup stays.
- Why use your own network (advantage)
  Description: Your phone doesn't have to switch networks to edit, and you keep your internet
             connection. On S3 boards it's also the way to update without a cable.
- Legacy WPA (TKIP) (compatibility)
  Description: Leave it off on current networks. Only turn it on if an old router really won't
             connect.
Notes:
- Your network's password is kept in the controller's protected memory and survives updates.

9.13. HOST — the USB Host and plugged-in devices
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SETTINGS › HOST
Purpose: The devices connected to the controller's USB HOST port: one card for each, with its MIDI
          channel and the preset editor.
Fields and items:
- USB HOST (status)
  Description: How many devices are connected, whether the module is responding and its firmware
             version (with a warning when it's outdated).
- Automatic detection (mode)
  Description: The USB Host recognizes what you plugged in on its own: regular USB MIDI pedals and
             the IK TONEX ONE work together, up to two devices on a hub. There's no mode to choose.
- One card per device (devices)
  Description: Manufacturer, product, hub port, USB ID, status and the channel that device listens
             on. Each TONEX ONE has its own channel; regular MIDI pedals share one filter. GP-5,
             TONEX ONE and Nano Cortex get the EDIT PRESET button.
- Translate Boss MS-3 (switch)
  Description: The MS-3 (and the Katana) don't understand regular parameter CCs: the host converts
             them to Boss's format. Only turn it on with them plugged in.
- Transmit-only mode (more pedals) (switch)
  Description: Lets the host send MIDI to up to four pedals on a hub, instead of two — in exchange,
             nothing comes back from the pedals: the tuner, the rig name, FOLLOW THE KEMPER and the
             GP-5 editor stop working. With a TONEX ONE plugged in, the limit stays at two.
- BFMiDi MIDI (host → controller) (switches)
  Description: Enable reverse MIDI repeats the MIDI coming from the plugged-in devices on the
             controller’s outputs (USB DEVICE and DIN5), like a thru/merge. BFMIDI MIDI Control Host
             lets a plugged-in device control the BFMiDi (call presets and banks, enter LIVE).
Notes:
- The HOST screen only works on models with USB Host: BFMIDI-3 and BFMIDI-S3.
- The Nano Cortex works on its own on the USB Host: it doesn't fit on a hub alongside another
   device.

9.14. BLUETOOTH — wireless
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SETTINGS › BLUETOOTH
Purpose: Bluetooth lives in the USB Host module and has three functions — the difference between
          them is who looks for whom.
Fields and items:
- KEYBOARD (function)
  Description: The controller becomes a Bluetooth keyboard: CCs become keys and shortcuts, to
             control programs that don't understand MIDI (sheet music, recorders, slides).
- MIDI (function)
  Description: It becomes a wireless MIDI interface called BFMiDi and waits: use it when the other
             device is the one doing the searching (phone, tablet, computer).
- PEDAL (function)
  Description: The controller looks for a Bluetooth MIDI pedal that's advertising and connects to
             it, so you get wireless footswitches.
- Bluetooth MIDI filter (channel)
  Description: Bluetooth has its own channel filter (OMNI lets everything through), separate from
             the USB devices' filter.

9.15. EDITOR — theme, saving, shortcuts and language
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SETTINGS › EDITOR
Purpose: Settings for the editor, not the controller: they stay on the device where you opened it.
Fields and items:
- THEME (selection)
  Description: LIGHT or DARK.
- AUTO SAVE (switch)
  Description: On (the default), the editor saves on its own less than a second after each change.
             Off, nothing is saved until you tap save — and leaving the preset discards the change.
- SHORTCUTS (switches)
  Description: One switch for each menu destination: the ones that are on become buttons on the MAIN
             card. STAGE MODE and the editors for plugged-in devices always appear, before your
             shortcuts.
- INTERFACE LANGUAGE (selection)
  Description: Português, English or Español. The controller's screen stays in English.

9.16. UPDATES — the controller
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SETTINGS › UPDATES — TAB 1
Purpose: Keep the BFMiDi up to date. The screen checks on its own when it opens and, if there's
          something new, one button takes care of everything.
How to:
1. Open SETTINGS › UPDATES. The check starts on its own.
2. If there's a new version, tap UPDATE THE BFMiDi (or UPDATE EVERYTHING, on boards without USB
   Host) and follow the screen.
3. Over the cable, the browser may ask you to authorize the controller's port in the middle of
   flashing — the card blinks to warn you and shows which one to choose. Don't unplug the cable
   until it's done.
Fields and items:
- The two tabs (boards with USB Host)
  Description: 1 BFMiDi is the controller; 2 USB HOST is the USB Host module. They're separate
             updates, done in that order.
- By cable or over the network (path)
  Description: By cable (Chrome or Edge on a computer, or the Mac and Windows apps) works on any
             model. Over the network (OTA) works on S3 boards with the pedal on your home network.
- AUTOMATIC BACKUP (switch)
  Description: Flashing by cable replaces the pedal's memory. With automatic backup (on by default),
             the editor saves your presets, images, icons and custom devices before flashing and
             puts everything back afterward. When it's off, the controller comes back with the
             factory package. Over the network nothing is erased.
- States and buttons (status)
  Description: BFMiDi up to date (or Equipment up to date, on boards without a USB Host), Update
             available or Not checked (no internet). CHECK AGAIN runs the check again; FORCE UPDATE
             reinstalls the current version. Your device does the downloading, not the pedal; a
             computer that has updated before keeps a copy and can update even without internet.
Notes:
- You can also update through the update site (Update BFMiDi), with the same automatic backup.

9.17. UPDATES — the USB Host
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SETTINGS › UPDATES — TAB 2
Purpose: The USB Host module has its own firmware. It's updated with the computer's cable plugged
          straight into the HOST port and the controller on its 9 V power supply — a wizard guides
          you step by step.
How to:
1. Update the controller first (tab 1).
2. On the 2 USB HOST tab, plug the 9 V power supply into the controller and tap UPDATE USB HOST.
3. Follow the wizard: it asks you to unplug the cable from the DEVICE port and connect the computer
   to the HOST port, then it flashes and checks.
4. At the end, put the cable back in the DEVICE port.
Fields and items:
- Why the 9 V power supply (required)
  Description: In this update the USB cable comes out of the DEVICE port and goes into HOST. Without
             the power supply, the BFMiDi turns off halfway through.
- What you need (list)
  Description: A 9 V pedal power supply, a USB cable with a USB-C end for the HOST port (ideally
             with the rectangular end on the computer side), Chrome or Edge on the computer (or the
             BFMiDi app for Windows/Mac) and about 3 minutes. Your presets aren't erased.
- If it stops halfway (rescue)
  Description: The wizard always tells you what happened — whether nothing changed or the firmware
             was left incomplete. If the flash is incomplete, the tab offers RECOVER; don't turn off
             the controller before trying again.

9.18. BACKUP — copy and restore
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SETTINGS › BACKUP
Purpose: Save your work to a file — the whole pedal, a bank or a preset — and bring it back when you
          need it.
Fields and items:
- FULL BACKUP (file)
  Description: All the presets. Choose whether to include images and icons (the file gets much
             bigger, but it's the real copy) and the global settings (board, colors, controls,
             custom devices). BACK UP downloads the file; RESTORE brings it back.
- EXPORT PERFORMANCE (one bank)
  Description: Exports or imports a whole bank — the six presets of a show — taking along only the
             images and icons it uses. When importing, you choose the destination bank, which gets
             replaced.
- SINGLE PRESET (one preset)
  Description: Exports or imports a single preset (no images), to take a song from one pedal to
             another.
Notes:
- Keep your files somewhere other than your phone (cloud, computer). A backup that lives only on the
   device that can break isn't a backup.

9.19. TESTS — diagnostics and MIDI monitor
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SETTINGS › TESTS
Purpose: Find out whether the problem is the cable, the channel or the command — without setting up
          any preset.
Fields and items:
- HARD TEST (diagnostics)
  Description: Three ten-second tests: LEDS, DISPLAY and MIDI. If the LEDs light up in the test, the
             problem is in the setup, not the hardware.
- MIDI SEND (tool)
  Description: Sends a PC or a CC right away, on the chosen channel. With AUTO SEND, every + or −
             sends immediately — it's the fastest way to find out which number your device responds
             to.
- MONITOR MIDI (diagnostics)
  Description: Shows what the controller sends on preset recall and on LIVE's live triggers, in
             order, with an option to copy.
Notes:
- If the manual send works and the preset doesn't, the problem is in the preset (channel or value) —
   not the cable or the device.

9.20. FACTORY RESET — erase and go back to defaults
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SETTINGS › FACTORY RESET
Purpose: Erase the presets or the global settings and start over. It's the red item on the menu, and
          for good reason.
How to:
1. Make a full backup first. There's no undo.
Fields and items:
- ERASE ALL PRESETS (irreversible)
  Description: All 60 presets go back to a blank default: footswitches in STOMP with no channel (so
             they don’t show on the screen) and layer 2 off. Images, icons and custom devices are
             not erased.
- ERASE GLOBAL CONFIG (irreversible)
  Description: Resets colors, brightness, auto start, banks, combos and controls to the defaults —
             and also the board model. Check the model in HARDWARE right afterward. Your home Wi-Fi
             network isn't erased.
- OFFLINE MODE (local copy)
  Description: In offline mode, RESET LOCAL COPY also appears; it returns the copy stored on your
             device to the factory package without touching the controller.
Notes:
- You don't need to reset before updating.

TOPIC 10 - ADVANCED FEATURES
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Page: 6
Summary: Commands the controller sends to itself, integrations with specific devices, and what to do
        when something doesn't respond.
Introduction: Nothing on this page is required to play. These features are for those who have
        already mastered the basics and want to get more out of the controller — or need to solve a
        specific problem.

10.1. Navigation commands (the controller talking to itself)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Purpose: Besides sending MIDI out, a footswitch can send a command to the controller itself — change
          bank, preset or layer without leaving LIVE.
How to:
1. In any footswitch CC list (STOMP, SINGLE, MACROS, MOMENTARY, SPIN, TAP TEMPO), scroll to the end:
   the navigation commands come after the regular CCs, marked with ».
Fields and items:
- » UP BANK and » DOWN BANK (command)
  Description: Move up or down one letter, calling up the preset in the new bank. Banks turned off
             in ACTIVE BANKS are skipped.
- » UP PRESET and » DOWN PRESET (command)
  Description: Step to the neighboring preset within the bank, cycling around (on four-footswitch
             boards, only among those four). It's “next song” with your foot, without leaving LIVE.
- » OUT LIVE MODE and » SW_LIVE (command)
  Description: OUT LIVE MODE leaves LIVE and goes back to preset navigation; SW_LIVE is the toggle:
             it goes in and out. On boards without a LIVE button, having one of these on a
             footswitch is what guarantees your way back.
- » TO LAYER (command)
  Description: Switches between the preset's Layer 1 and Layer 2. It only makes sense inside LIVE,
             with Layer 2 turned on.
- Preview navigation (external footswitches) (command)
  Description: The external footswitches have two commands of their own, » UP BANK STANDBY and »
             DOWN BANK STANDBY: they move up and down through banks in preview, without changing the
             sound, and wait for a preset footswitch to confirm.
Notes:
- These commands don't go out over the MIDI cable: the controller itself uses them. RAMP and STEPS
   don't offer them.

10.2. Device integrations
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Purpose: Some devices talk to the controller beyond regular MIDI. Choose the device in Friendly Mode
          to turn these features on.
Fields and items:
- Kemper Player (integration)
  Description: With the controller's DEVICE port connected to the Player's USB: the rig name on the
             screen (GET NAMES), the tuner on the controller's screen (a footswitch sending CC 31),
             FOLLOW THE KEMPER and the rig editor (EDIT RIG). The command list gains the Kemper's
             internal parameters by name (marked with ◆), and values such as Transpose show up as
             musical notes.
- Valeton GP-5 (integration)
  Description: Plugged into the USB Host: the command list gains the GP-5's internal parameters by
             name — the same as turning its knobs from the controller. In TAP TEMPO, a CC 119 target
             sends the delay time in milliseconds (up to 1 s), so the tempo is right from the first
             beat. Plus the full preset editor.
- IK TONEX ONE (integration)
  Description: Plugged into the USB Host, it's recognized automatically: preset changes through each
             BFMiDi preset's PC, a channel of its own for each TONEX (up to two on a hub) and the
             preset editor.
- Neural DSP Nano Cortex (integration)
  Description: Plugged into the USB Host on its own: the tuner on the controller's screen (a
             footswitch sending CC 43 on the Nano's channel), the Nano's preset name on the screen
             (GET NAMES, turned on in the Friendly Mode card) and the preset editor. With Cortex
             Cloud connected over Bluetooth, the Nano refuses to talk over USB.
- BOSS MS-3 and Katana (integration)
  Description: With Translate Boss MS-3 turned on (SETTINGS › HOST), the USB Host converts commands
             into Boss's own format.
Notes:
- The preset editors are explained in the Device preset editors chapter.

10.3. When something doesn't respond
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Purpose: A short checklist to find the problem in the right order, from the most likely to the
          rarest.
Fields and items:
- The effects unit doesn't change sound (checklist)
  Description: 1. Does the cable go from the BFMiDi to the effects unit (MIDI OUT → MIDI IN)?
             2. Is the preset's channel the one the effects unit listens to?
             3. Use MIDI SEND in SETTINGS › TESTS: if it works there, the problem is in the preset;
             if it doesn't work even there, it's the cable or the device.
             4. Does the effects unit miss the first messages after a preset change? Use SEND TIMING
             in SETTINGS › HARDWARE.
- I can't open the editor (checklist)
  Description: 1. The pedal's Wi-Fi turns itself off 90 seconds after power-up if nobody connects:
             turn the controller off and on again.
             2. The network is BFMIDI_WIFI, password bfmidi@editor.
             3. Type the address with http:// (http://192.168.4.1 or http://bfmidi.local) — without
             it, the browser searches Google.
             4. On a Mac (macOS 15+), give the browser Local Network permission.
             5. Last resort: USB cable + Chrome/Edge, or the Mac and Windows apps.
- A footswitch does nothing (checklist)
  Description: Check that it isn't set to MUTE and that you're in the right mode (PRESET × LIVE). In
             PRESET mode, look at PRESET CALL: the gesture may be set to NONE, and ENTER LIVE MODE
             only works on the active preset's footswitch. An open Kemper editor pauses the
             footswitches (hold one for 2 s to exit). Also check the model in HARDWARE.
- The footswitch icon disappeared from the screen (checklist)
  Description: Its function has no MIDI channel: a footswitch without a channel doesn't show up on
             the controller's screen. Give the command a channel (or use FAVORITE, which shows up
             even without a channel).
- I can't upload images (checklist)
  Description: Check the memory bar in SETTINGS › IMAGES: images and icons share the same space.
             Delete what you don't use, check the file size (up to 50 KB per image, 30 KB per icon)
             and use the USB cable when you can.
- The pedal's screen went dark after changing the model (checklist)
  Description: The wrong model leaves the screen and the footswitches unresponsive. Connect over
             Wi-Fi or the cable and choose the right model in SETTINGS › HARDWARE — including after
             ERASE GLOBAL CONFIG, which resets the model to the default.
Notes:
- Before changing anything big, make a backup. It's what turns a mistake into five lost minutes
   instead of a lost night.
