TOPIC 1 - OPENING THE EDITOR
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Page: 1
Summary: The editor lives inside the controller. You open it over its own Wi-Fi, over your home
        network, or through a USB cable — nothing to install.
Introduction: The BFMiDi carries its own configuration app. There's no program to download: the
        controller creates a Wi-Fi network, you join it and open an address in the browser. It's the
        same editor on a phone, a tablet or a computer.

1.1. Over the controller's Wi-Fi (the normal path)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SETTINGS › WIFI
Purpose: Four steps, nothing to install: turn the pedal on, join the network it creates, type an
          address in the browser and you're done — the editor opens.
How to:
1. Turn the controller on. Its Wi-Fi comes up by itself at boot and stays on for 90 seconds waiting
   for someone to connect.
2. On your phone, open Settings › Wi-Fi and tap BFMIDI_WIFI. The password is bfmidi@editor.
3. If your device warns that the network “has no internet”, that's correct — stay connected. The
   editor lives in the pedal, not on the internet.
4. Open the browser and type 192.168.4.1 in the address bar. The editor loads immediately.
Fields and items:
- The BFMIDI_WIFI network (Wi-Fi)
  Description: This is the network the controller itself creates (AP mode). It shows up in your
             phone's Wi-Fi list next to your home networks. The password is always bfmidi@editor,
             all lowercase.
- The address 192.168.4.1 (browser)
  Description: Type it straight into the address bar — just numbers and dots, no “www” and no
             “https”. If the browser insists on searching instead of opening it, write
             http://192.168.4.1.
             Tip: use the browser's “Add to Home Screen” to leave an app-like shortcut.
- Using your home network (optional)
  Description: You can put the controller on your network: in SETTINGS › WIFI, tap SCAN, pick the
             network, type the password and CONNECT.
             From then on you reach it at http://bfmidi.local (or the IP shown on screen) without
             switching networks on your phone — much more comfortable day to day.
             Only 2.4 GHz networks appear: that's a chip limitation, not a firmware one.
- The Wi-Fi turned itself off (help)
  Description: With nobody connected, the radio switches off after 90 seconds — it saves power and
             avoids leaving an open network on stage.
             To bring it back: power the controller off and on (Wi-Fi comes up at boot). If you'd
             like a foot shortcut, set a combo to the WI-FI ON/OFF action in SETTINGS › BANKS.
Notes:
- It works the same on a phone, tablet or computer — it's always the same screen.
- The controller's Wi-Fi password never changes, and it isn't your home network's password.

1.2. Over the USB cable (computer)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Purpose: On a computer you can talk to the controller over the cable, with no radio at all. It's the
          most stable path for long configuration sessions — and the only one that works with Wi-Fi
          off.
How to:
1. Use Chrome or Edge on the computer (Safari gives no serial port access).
2. Plug the controller's USB cable into the computer.
3. Open the editor and choose the USB connection; the browser will ask you to authorize the port.
Fields and items:
- When the cable is better (when to use)
  Description: When the house Wi-Fi is unstable, when you're going to change a lot at once, when you
             need to send images and icons (which are large files), and whenever you're going to
             update the controller.
- iPhone and iPad (limitation)
  Description: Safari doesn't open serial ports, so on iPhone/iPad use Wi-Fi. It isn't a controller
             limitation — no app gets that access on iOS.
Notes:
- When connected by cable, the editor shows USB in the header corner instead of the Wi-Fi icon.

1.3. Android app
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Purpose: On Android there's a dedicated app that opens the editor fullscreen, with no browser bar.
          The screen ships inside the app; it talks to the controller over Wi-Fi.
How to:
1. Download: BFMiDi for Android (APK) — the link always gives you the latest version.
2. Install: open the downloaded file and allow “install apps from unknown sources” once.
3. Use it: connect the phone to the controller's Wi-Fi and open the app — it finds the pedal by
   itself.
Fields and items:
- Updating the app (maintenance)
  Description: When a new version comes out, download the APK from the same link and tap Update — no
             uninstalling, nothing lost.
             The app's screen is updated by the app itself. The controller's firmware is still
             updated from SETTINGS › UPDATE.
- iPhone and iPad (alternative)
  Description: There's no APK, and none is needed: open 192.168.4.1 in Safari and use “Add to Home
             Screen”. The shortcut opens fullscreen, just like an app.
Notes:
- It's the same editor as in the browser, just embedded — that's why it loads instantly.

TOPIC 2 - SETUP WIZARD
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Page: 1
Summary: The guided path: one question per screen, in plain language, until the preset is ready —
        including the LIVE mode footswitches.
Introduction: If you've never dealt with MIDI, start here. The wizard asks one thing at a time and
        builds the preset for you. You don't need to know what CC, PC or channel mean to reach the
        end — and what it builds is a normal preset, which you can later tweak in the editor like
        any other.
        It sits at the bottom of the MAIN card, on the PRESET screen.

2.1. How the wizard works
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: WIZARD — ONE QUESTION PER SCREEN
Purpose: It runs through five stages — pedal, footswitch, name, sound and LIVE mode — and only saves
          at the end of each block. You can quit halfway: whatever was saved stays saved.
How to:
1. On the PRESET screen, tap WIZARD · GUIDED SETUP, at the bottom of the MAIN card.
2. Tell it which pedal you have. That's what makes the editor show commands by name instead of
   number.
3. Step on the footswitch you want to set up — the controller figures out which one it was.
4. Give the preset a name, choose which sound it calls and move on to LIVE mode, one footswitch at a
   time.
Fields and items:
- Stage 1 — Which pedal you have (choice)
  Description: You pick your device from a searchable list (Ampero, Kemper, GP-5, HX Stomp and many
             others). That's what switches on Friendly Mode: from there on, commands appear by name
             — “Drive”, “Delay”, “Reverb” — instead of bare numbers.
             Can't find yours? Choose GENERIC and carry on: everything works the same, just with
             numbers.
- Stage 2 — Step on the footswitch (gesture)
  Description: Instead of hunting for “bank A, preset 3” in a list, you step on the footswitch you
             want to configure. The wizard continues with that one.
             To do that it puts the controller in PRESET mode for a moment — the mode where stepping
             changes preset.
- Stages 3 and 4 — Name and sound (fields)
  Description: The name is what shows on the controller's display — usually the song's name.
             The sound is which preset of your effects unit this button will call. With Friendly
             Mode on, it appears with the name the unit uses (on the Ampero, A01-1, A01-2…) instead
             of a number.
- Stage 5 — LIVE mode, one footswitch at a time (sequence)
  Description: Before asking anything, the wizard explains with a drawing what LIVE mode is —
             because “LIVE” means nothing to someone building their first preset.
             Then, for each footswitch, it asks one screen at a time: behaviour → command → LED
             colour → initial state → on-screen colour → icon and name. And when each footswitch is
             done, it shows a recap with the preset taking shape.
- The four behaviours offered (choice)
  Description: ON / OFF — the most common: your foot switches an effect on and off, like a real
             pedal. TAP TEMPO — you tap the tempo with your foot. ONE COMMAND — every tap sends the
             same thing. NOTHING — the footswitch is left with no function.
             These are four of the controller's ten modes: the wizard is the front door, not the
             ceiling. The other six are available in the normal editor.
- Two colours, two questions (detail)
  Description: The LED ring colour (what your foot finds without looking) and the on-screen tile
             colour are different things — hence two screens.
             To save you work, the screen colour follows the LED one until you touch it: if you just
             click through you end with both matching, and if you want them different, the option is
             there.
- Initial state (choice)
  Description: It only appears in the ON / OFF behaviour, and the question is: does the effect come
             in already on when you call up the song, or does it wait for you to step on it?
             It isn't decoration. The answer changes what goes out the MIDI cable along with the
             preset — not just the LED colour.
Notes:
- The wizard doesn't undo fine-tuning you've already done: when saving the footswitch's display it
   touches the label, the icon and the on-state colour, and leaves the rest as it was.
- Prefer to build it by hand? The next section shows the same path without the wizard.

TOPIC 3 - MY FIRST PRESET
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Page: 1
Summary: What a preset is, how to tell the effects unit which sound to load, save and test with your
        foot.
Introduction: Think of it this way: your effects unit already has several sounds saved; the BFMiDi
        is the remote control that, with a tap of your foot, makes it jump to whichever sound you
        want. A BFMiDi preset is one of those shortcuts.
        There are 10 banks (A to J) with 6 presets each — 60 shortcuts in total.

3.1. The four steps
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: THE MAIN CARD
Purpose: Choose the preset, say which sound it calls, save and step on it. That alone gets the
          controller playing.
How to:
1. In the left column, tap preset 1 of bank A. It gets highlighted — that's the one you're editing.
2. In the MAIN card, choose the PC (your effects unit's sound) and check the CHANNEL. When in doubt,
   channel 1.
3. Write a name at the top of the card — it's what shows on the controller's display.
4. Tap SAVE on the bottom bar. Then step on footswitch 1: the effects unit jumps to the chosen
   sound.
Fields and items:
- PC — the effects unit's sound (selection)
  Description: PC stands for Program Change: it's the sound's number on your effects unit, the same
             one shown on its display. With Friendly Mode on, the list shows each sound's name
             instead of the number.
- CHANNEL (selection)
  Description: The MIDI channel is “which device listens”. Most effects units come on channel 1. If
             you have more than one device in the chain, each sits on its own channel and the preset
             can talk to all of them.
             OFF means this preset sends no PC at all — useful when it only exists to set up LIVE
             mode.
- Preset name (text)
  Description: Up to 16 characters, and it's only a label for the controller's display — the effects
             unit never sees this name. Usually it's the song's name.
- The sound didn't change? (help)
  Description: Check three things, in this order: (1) the cable runs from the BFMiDi to the effects
             unit (MIDI OUT → MIDI IN); (2) the preset's channel matches what the unit listens to;
             (3) you tapped SAVE before stepping on it.
             If still nothing, use MIDI TRIGGER in SETTINGS › TESTS: it sends a command on the spot
             and shows whether the unit responds.
Notes:
- Nothing is stored before SAVE: leaving the preset without saving discards the changes. If you
   prefer, turn on auto-save in SETTINGS › EDITOR.
- Repeat for presets 2, 3, 4… and then for the other banks. A bank is usually a gig; a preset, a
   song.

TOPIC 4 - HARDWARE
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Page: 1
Summary: Physical hardware connections by model: USB, MIDI, expression, dual switch and power ports.

4.1. What connects where
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: PHYSICAL CONNECTIONS
Purpose: Select your controller model to view the physical hardware connections. The available ports
          vary by version.
Fields and items:
- Controller model (selection)
  Description: Choose the physical model of your BFMiDi. The connection diagram changes depending on
             the controller, because not all of them have USB Host, EXP, Dual Switch or the same
             connector layout.
             New in v13: the family gained the ESP32-S3 line — the BFMIDI-3 8SW+ (8 footswitches)
             and the BFMIDI-3 NANO+ V2. These boards have a new processor, more memory and more room
             for images and icons. The editor only offers models compatible with the processor of
             the pedal you are connected to, so you cannot pick the wrong board.
- Top/front view (drawing)
  Description: The selected model's panel seen from above: the position of the footswitches (LIVE,
             GLOBAL and SW1–SW6), the display and the indicators. Models without a drawing yet show
             the reserved space.
- Rear/connectors view (drawing)
  Description: The rear panel with the model's physical ports — on the BFMIDI-1 7S: MIDI TRS OUT, 9v
             power (center-negative) and USB DEVICE. Each port is detailed in the fields below.
- USB Device (port)
  Description: The main USB port — the controller appears as a USB MIDI device on the
             computer/tablet. The same port also handles the cable editor (Web Serial, no WiFi) and
             firmware updates through the browser.
- USB Host (port)
  Description: The opposite of the USB Device: here you plug devices INTO the controller — MIDI
             keyboards, another BFMiDi, USB effects units. Managed in the SYSTEM › CONEXÕES › USB
             HOST tab. It comes in two versions: standard USB Host (BFMIDI-2) forwards the connected
             device's MIDI; USB Host Plus (BFMIDI-3) adds the Tonex mode (to control the IK Tonex
             One), reverse MIDI / Control Host (the plugged device controls the BFMiDi) and
             Bluetooth (BLE keyboard).
- MIDI TRS (port)
  Description: MIDI output on a minijack (TRS) connector, for pedals with a compact MIDI input. It
             carries the same signal as the DIN5 output — use the cable/adapter suited to your
             pedal.
- MIDI DIN (port)
  Description: The classic 5-pin MIDI output, for effects units and traditional gear.
- Expression pedal (EXP) (port)
  Description: ("+" models.) Input for an analog expression pedal (TRS) — the movement becomes a
             continuous CC from 0 to 127. Setup and calibration in GLOBAL › MIDI.
- Dual Switch (port)
  Description: ("+" models.) Input for an external dual pedal (2 simple footswitches, no LED) — each
             button gets its own function in GLOBAL › MIDI.
- Power (DC IN) (port)
  Description: The controller's power input. Use the power supply recommended by BFFX — if in doubt,
             check the label next to your device's connector.
Notes:
- The available ports vary by model: standard USB Host on the BFMIDI-2 and Plus (Tonex, reverse
   MIDI, Bluetooth) on the BFMIDI-3; EXP and Dual Switch only on the "+" variants.

TOPIC 5 - THE EDITOR SCREEN
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Page: 2
Summary: Three columns: where you pick the preset, where you set what it sends, and where you see
        how the controller will look.
Introduction: This is the screen where you'll spend most of your time. On the left, the bank and its
        six presets. In the middle, the MAIN card — what this preset sends. On the right, a preview
        of what will show on the controller's display. At the bottom, the bar with navigation and
        SAVE.
        On a phone the three columns become a single stacked one, in the same order.

5.1. Overview of the three columns
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: PRESET SCREEN — FULL
Purpose: Grasp at a glance where everything lives before touching any field.
Fields and items:
- Column 1 — bank and presets (navigation)
  Description: It shows the bank letter prominently and its six presets with each one's name.
             Tapping a preset is choosing who you're editing — and the controller jumps to it right
             away, so you can hear it.
- Column 2 — the MAIN card (editing)
  Description: The preset's heart: name, PC, CHANNEL and the buttons that open Layer 2, Display and
             Extras. It's also where the WIZARD shortcut lives.
- Column 3 — the display preview (visual)
  Description: A faithful drawing of what the controller will show, with PRESET and LIVE tabs so you
             can see both moments. Below it sit the POSITION, APPEARANCE and SCREEN COLOURS
             settings.
- PRESET MODE × LIVE MODE (switch)
  Description: The screen's most important switch. In PRESET MODE you edit what the preset calls up;
             in LIVE MODE, what each footswitch does inside that preset.
             It also switches the actual controller's mode — that's why the pedal changes screen
             when you use it.
- L1 / L2 badge (Layer) (indicator)
  Description: Each preset can have two layers of LIVE functions — as if the six footswitches had
             two pages. The badge shows which one you're editing, and stays inactive until Layer 2
             is enabled on that preset.
Notes:
- The previews in this manual are the editor's real markup: what you see here is exactly what
   appears on the app's screen.

5.2. Choosing bank and preset
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: BANK AND PRESETS
Purpose: The left column is the controller's map: ten letters, six presets in each. It's how you
          move through the set.
How to:
1. Tap the highlighted letter to change bank (A → B → C…).
2. Tap one of the six presets to edit it. The controller follows along.
Fields and items:
- The bank letter (navigation)
  Description: There are ten banks, A to J. A bank is usually a gig, a set or a band; the six
             presets inside it, the songs.
             Banks you don't use can be switched off in SETTINGS › BANKS — navigation then skips
             straight over them.
- The six presets (selection)
  Description: Each square is a preset, with the name you gave it. The lit one is what you're
             editing — and what the controller is playing at that moment.
             On four-footswitch boards, presets 5 and 6 still exist and remain editable; they just
             can't be reached by foot.
Notes:
- Switching presets here discards unsaved changes. If that bothers you, turn on auto-save in
   SETTINGS › EDITOR.

5.3. Layer 2, Display and Extras
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: EXTRA SENDS OPEN
Purpose: Three buttons on the MAIN card open what doesn't fit on the main screen: the second
          footswitch layer, this preset's appearance and additional MIDI sends.
How to:
1. Layer 2 — enables this preset's second layer of LIVE functions.
2. Display — opens this preset's appearance settings on the display.
3. Extras — adds MIDI sends beyond the main PC.
Fields and items:
- Extra sends (up to 12) (list)
  Description: Besides the main PC, the preset can send up to 12 more messages when called — each
             with its own type (PC or CC), number, value and channel.
             This is how you set the whole scene with one tap: change the unit's sound, switch the
             delay on, set another device's volume and change the desk's scene, all in the same
             preset.
- EXP value — expression pedal in this preset (special value)
  Description: On a CC extra send, the value list ends with EXP (EXPRESSION PEDAL). Choose it and
             the row sends no MIDI: it says where the expression pedal points while this preset is
             active.
             The CC and channel set in SETTINGS › FOOTSWITCHES become just the default — a preset
             with an EXP row replaces that destination. You can have more than one, if you want the
             same pedal driving two destinations at once.
- External SWs on the display (switch)
  Description: On boards with an external footswitch input, this switch decides whether the ESW1 and
             ESW2 indicators appear on the display in this preset. Their colours and positions are
             global, in SETTINGS › FOOTSWITCHES.

5.4. The display preview and the preset's appearance
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: DISPLAY PREVIEW
Purpose: See, before stepping on anything, exactly what the controller will show — in both moments:
          calling the preset and playing in LIVE.
How to:
1. Use the PRESET and LIVE tabs at the top of the preview to switch between the two screens.
2. Open POSITION, APPEARANCE or SCREEN COLOURS to adjust; the preview updates as you go.
Fields and items:
- POSITION (layout)
  Description: Chooses this preset's screen arrangement — how many tiles appear and how they're laid
             out. There are fixed layouts (1 to 4), LIST mode (which shows presets in sequence) and
             CUSTOM, where you drag each icon wherever you want and set its size.
             Left alone, the preset follows the global layout set in SETTINGS › DISPLAY. Choose here
             and this preset gets its own — handy for a song that needs four big effects while the
             others use the list.
- APPEARANCE (text)
  Description: How the preset's name is written on the display: font size, bold, alignment and
             position on screen. It's what makes the song's name readable from the stage.
- SCREEN COLOURS (colours)
  Description: Colour of the name, the border and the background — and the background can be a
             colour, a gradient or an image you uploaded in SETTINGS › IMAGES.
             There are two independent backgrounds: one for the PRESET screen and one for LIVE,
             because you usually want different things in the two moments.
Notes:
- The preview is faithful to what the pedal draws, including icons, colours and text size — if it
   looks good here, it looks good there.

5.5. The bottom bar (navigate, copy and save)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: BOTTOM BAR
Purpose: Four buttons that apply to the whole screen: home, settings, the “+” copy-and-paste menu,
          and SAVE.
Fields and items:
- Home (navigation)
  Description: Returns to the preset editing screen, from wherever you are.
- Settings (navigation)
  Description: Opens the menu with the fourteen destinations of configuration — from FRIENDLY MODE
             to RESTORE. That's the subject of this manual's next page.
- The “+” menu (copy and paste) (actions)
  Description: Copies and pastes whole presets, layers and banks. It's the shortcut that avoids
             rebuilding a song similar to another from scratch: copy the preset, paste it at the
             destination and adjust what differs.
             Inside LIVE mode, it also copies and pastes one footswitch's setup onto another.
- SAVE (action)
  Description: Writes the changes to the controller's memory. Nothing is stored before that — unless
             you turned on auto-save in SETTINGS › EDITOR.
             The small dot in the button's corner warns you when a change is pending.

TOPIC 6 - LIVE MODE
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Page: 3
Summary: Inside each preset, the six footswitches become effect pedals. This is where the controller
        stops being just a sound selector.
Introduction: The controller has two moments. In PRESET mode, stepping on a footswitch calls up a
        song. In LIVE mode, you're already inside a song and each footswitch controls something in
        it: switch the drive on, tap the delay's tempo, push the solo volume up.
        LIVE mode is configured per preset: the same six feet do different things in each song. And
        each preset can have two layers (L1 and L2), giving twelve functions per song.

6.1. How to configure a footswitch
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: LIVE MODE SCREEN
Purpose: Flip the switch to LIVE MODE, choose which footswitch you want to configure and say what it
          does. Always in that order.
How to:
1. At the top of the screen, switch from PRESET MODE to LIVE MODE.
2. In the left column, tap the footswitch (SW1 to SW6) you want to configure.
3. Tap the operation mode field and choose one of the ten modes.
4. Fill in that mode's fields and tap SAVE.
Fields and items:
- The footswitch grid (selection)
  Description: The six squares on the left stop being presets and become this preset's footswitches,
             each showing the icon and label that will appear on the display.
             The lit one is what you're configuring.
- L1 and L2 — the two layers (layers)
  Description: Turning Layer 2 on in the MAIN card gives the preset a second page of functions for
             the same six feet. You switch layers during the show with a footswitch set up for it,
             or with the LIVE button on boards that have one.
             That's how you get twelve functions in a song without needing twelve footswitches.
- Entering and leaving LIVE mode on the pedal (on stage)
  Description: It depends on the model. Boards with a dedicated LIVE button: one tap enters (and
             cycles the layer), holding exits.
             Boards without it: use a two-footswitch combo with the SW_LIVE action (SETTINGS ›
             BANKS), or leave one preset footswitch with the EXIT TO BANK command. What matters is
             always having a way back.
Notes:
- Configuring LIVE mode is optional: a preset that only calls the sound is already useful and
   already plays.

6.2. The ten operation modes
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: MODE SELECTOR
Purpose: Each footswitch runs one mode. The mode decides what happens when you step on it — and it's
          the choice that changes everything else.
How to:
1. Tap the mode field, at the top of the footswitch panel, to open this list.
Fields and items:
- Which mode to choose (summary)
  Description: STOMP — switches an effect on and off. It's what 90% of people use.
             TAP TEMPO — you tap the tempo with your foot.
             SINGLE — every tap sends the same thing.
             MOMENTARY — active only while your foot is down.
             MACROS — one tap sends several commands at once.
             SPIN — cycles through three values.
             RAMP — sweeps smoothly from one value to another.
             STEPS — sequencer: sweeps four values in a loop.
             CONTROL — A/B selector between two destinations.
             MUTE — the footswitch is left with no function.
- Does changing mode erase what I had? (detail)
  Description: No. Each mode's parameters are stored separately: if you try SPIN and go back to
             STOMP, STOMP still has what you had configured before.
Notes:
- STEPS and CONTROL apply to footswitches SW1–SW6. The global buttons and external switches use the
   other eight modes.

6.3. STOMP — on and off
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SW — STOMP MODE
Purpose: The classic mode: your foot switches an effect on and off, like a real pedal. A single
          STOMP can have up to three sections, each on a different foot gesture.
How to:
1. Set up the SHORT CLICK section: choose the effect's CC and CHANNEL.
2. If you want more functions on the same foot, also set up LONG CLICK and RECLICK.
3. Set the LED colour and the initial state in OPTIONS.
Fields and items:
- SHORT CLICK / LONG CLICK / RECLICK (sections)
  Description: Three functions on the same footswitch, each on a gesture: a quick tap, a held tap
             and a repeated tap.
             The behaviour adapts to what you fill in: only the first section = classic stomp (and
             holding becomes momentary); two sections = two effects; three = three.
- CC or PC (button)
  Description: The button next to the number switches the message type: CC (normal for switching
             effects) or PC (to call up a sound).
- CUSTOM — your own values (button)
  Description: Normally on sends 127 and off sends 0. CUSTOM opens two knobs so you can choose other
             values — needed on devices that use, say, 64 for on.
- FAVORITE (button)
  Description: Turns the section into a shortcut to another preset: instead of sending a CC,
             stepping loads the bank, preset and mode you choose. Use it to “jump to the next song”
             without leaving LIVE.
- Options — trigger, initial state and LED (group)
  Description: Fire with preset: sends the command when the preset is called, without waiting for
             your foot. Starts ON: sets whether the effect comes in enabled. Invert LED: lights when
             off instead of on — useful when the plugin uses inverted logic.
             Beside it, the ring picks that footswitch's LED colour.
- SINGLE RING (switch)
  Description: With two or three sections, the LED ring normally splits — one colour per section.
             Turning SINGLE RING on lights all three pixels together, in the colour of the last
             section you touched.
             Useful when what matters is seeing from a distance which effect just changed, rather
             than the state of all three at once.
Notes:
- When in doubt, start with SHORT CLICK only. The other sections can be added later, without redoing
   anything.

6.4. TAP TEMPO — tapping the tempo
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SW — TAP TEMPO MODE
Purpose: You tap the song's tempo with your foot and the controller sends the timing to the delay
          (or whatever you want).
How to:
1. Choose your device's tap CC and the channel.
2. Tap SAVE and tap the tempo with your foot, in time with the song.
Fields and items:
- BPM on screen (visual)
  Description: When you tap, the controller shows the BPM in a temporary card on the display. How
             long it stays — and whether it shows the average or the last two taps — is set in
             SETTINGS › DISPLAY.
             Turning that card off doesn't erase the tempo: it still applies for whatever uses it.
- What else uses the beat (integration)
  Description: The tempo you tap is stored and used by STEPS mode when it's in TAP SYNC: tapping a
             new tempo changes the rhythm of the sequence already playing.

6.5. SINGLE — one command per tap
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SW — SINGLE MODE
Purpose: Every tap sends the same message. No on/off state, no alternating — it's a pure command
          button.
How to:
1. Choose the command (CC or PC), the value and the channel.
Fields and items:
- When to use it (use)
  Description: For commands that have no two states: call a scene, fire a sample, advance the score,
             reset a looper. It's also the right mode for the controller's own navigation commands
             (change bank or preset without leaving LIVE).

6.6. MOMENTARY — only while your foot is down
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SW — MOMENTARY MODE
Purpose: On when you press, off when you release. It's a doorbell button, not a switch.
How to:
1. Choose the CC and channel; adjust the pressed and released values if your device doesn't use
   127/0.
Fields and items:
- What it's for (use)
  Description: Effects you want for a passage rather than the whole song: a kill switch, a fixed
             wah, a freeze, a two-note boost. Lift your foot and it's over.

6.7. MACROS — several commands in one tap
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SW — MACROS MODE
Purpose: A single foot sends a sequence of messages at once — and sends everything back when
          switched off.
How to:
1. Fill the slots with the commands that should go out together (each with type, number, value and
   channel).
2. If you like, use the three gesture sections, as in STOMP.
Fields and items:
- The typical case (example)
  Description: The “chorus switch”: one tap turns the drive on, pushes the delay up, drops the
             chorus and raises the volume — four devices, four messages, one foot. Switch it off and
             everything returns.

6.8. SPIN — cycling three values
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SW — SPIN MODE
Purpose: Each tap advances to the next of three values, cycling. The LED ring shows which one you're
          on.
How to:
1. Choose the CC and channel, and set the three values (PX 1, PX 2 and PX 3).
2. If you like, use HOLD to send a different command when you hold your foot down.
Fields and items:
- The three states (cycle)
  Description: Each tap cycles PX 1 → PX 2 → PX 3 → PX 1, and the LED lights the current state's
             pixel in the chosen colour. Use it for three gain levels, three delay times, three
             positions of a parameter.

6.9. RAMP — sweeping smoothly
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SW — RAMP MODE
Purpose: Instead of jumping from one value to another, the controller travels the whole way — as if
          you turned the knob by hand.
How to:
1. Set the range (minimum and maximum) and the rise and fall times.
2. Choose the curve (LINEAR, EXP, LOG or SINE) and the behaviour (TOGGLE, HOLD or LOOP).
Fields and items:
- TOGGLE, HOLD and LOOP (behaviour)
  Description: TOGGLE: each tap flips the direction (up, then down). HOLD: rises while your foot is
             down and returns when you lift it. LOOP: keeps going back and forth on its own, without
             stopping.
- The curve (selection)
  Description: Changes how the value travels: LINEAR at constant speed, EXP slow at first, LOG fast
             at first, SINE smooth at both ends. It makes a real difference on volume and filters.
Notes:
- RAMP only works inside LIVE mode — it doesn't fire when the preset is called.

6.10. STEPS — CC sequencer
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SW — STEPS MODE
Purpose: When on, the footswitch sweeps four values on a single CC, looping by itself — until you
          turn it off. It's what you'd do by turning a knob in time with the music.
How to:
1. Choose the sequence's CC and channel and fill in the four values.
2. Choose the timing: MANUAL (a time in milliseconds per step) or TAP SYNC (a musical figure per
   step, over the beat).
3. Optionally turn on the effect: a second CC that gets ON when it starts and OFF when it stops.
Fields and items:
- The four values (sequence)
  Description: The sequence runs through the values in order, forever: 0 → 40 → 90 → 127 → 0 → …
             When you switch it on, the first step goes out immediately; when you switch it off, it
             stops at once, without finishing the cycle. And restarting begins at the first.
- MANUAL × TAP SYNC (timing)
  Description: In MANUAL, each step has its own time in milliseconds — you can build uneven rhythms.
             In TAP SYNC, each step gets a musical figure measured against the beat: tap a new tempo
             on TAP TEMPO and the sequence changes with it. The beat can come from the tap or be a
             typed BPM — and with a typed BPM this switch ignores the tap on purpose, allowing a
             fixed rhythm here while another foot taps the delay's tempo.
- The effect (optional) (send)
  Description: A second CC, with its own channel, that gets ON when the sequence starts and OFF when
             it stops — usually to switch on the effect being modulated. The channel is what enables
             it: leave it OFF and the mode becomes a pure sequencer.
Notes:
- Like RAMP, STEPS only operates in LIVE mode and doesn't fire when the preset is called.
- The LED blinks in time with the sequence, so you can see the tempo without looking at the effects
   unit.

6.11. CONTROL — A/B selector
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SW — CONTROL MODE
Purpose: One click alternating two destinations: the one coming in gets 127, the one going out gets
          0. They're never both on nor both off.
How to:
1. In the FX 1 tab, choose the first destination's CC, channel and LED colour.
2. In the FX 2 tab, do the same for the second.
3. Choose which of the two starts active when the preset is called.
Fields and items:
- When to use CONTROL instead of STOMP (concept)
  Description: Use CONTROL when the two effects are alternatives: two amps, two channels, two
             cabinets — cases where having both on makes no sense.
             Use a two-section STOMP when they're independent: a drive and a delay that can be on
             together, off together, or just one. In STOMP they also live on different gestures;
             here they share the same click.
- Colours and the display (visual)
  Description: Each destination has its own colour, and since there's no “off” state, the ring never
             goes dark — it changes colour. The name and icon on screen can also differ per
             destination.
Notes:
- The 0 of the outgoing destination goes first: on devices where both share the same block, sending
   the 127 first would leave both on for an instant.

6.12. MUTE — footswitch with no function
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SW — MUTE MODE
Purpose: The mode for when you want nothing on that foot. It sends no MIDI, lights no LED, shows
          nothing on the display.
Fields and items:
- Why it exists (use)
  Description: It's the default mode for a footswitch you haven't configured yet — and the way to
             disable a foot that was in use, without erasing the rest of the preset. A MUTE foot
             simply does nothing when stepped on.

6.13. Each footswitch's appearance
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Purpose: Icon, label and colours of the tile representing that footswitch on the controller's
          display — in both on and off states.
How to:
1. In the footswitch panel, scroll to the bottom: PREVIEW, BACKGROUND, BORDER, NAME COLOUR, ICON /
   LABEL and FONT SIZE.
2. Each colour has two columns: OFF and ON. That's what makes the effect “light up” on screen.
Fields and items:
- Icon and label (content)
  Description: The icon comes from a built-in library (pedals, effects, symbols) and you can also
             upload your own in SETTINGS › IMAGES. The label is the short text below — “DRIVE”,
             “DLY”, “SOLO”.
- FONT SIZE (selection)
  Description: AUTO lets the controller choose based on space; or fix it at 9, 12, 18 or 24 pt when
             you want to guarantee readability from a distance.
             Choosing a fixed size, it applies even in small-tile layouts. And if the text doesn't
             fit, the controller steps down one notch at a time until it does — a clipped label on
             stage reads as a fault.
             The size belongs to the whole footswitch: the tile has a single name, and a size per
             state would make the text jump on every tap.
- OFF and ON colours (colours)
  Description: Background, border and name colour, each with a value for off and one for on. Well
             chosen, they make the effect's state readable from a distance, with no need to look at
             the effects unit.
Notes:
- In modes with more than one destination (multi-section STOMP, CONTROL), each section has its own
   icon and colour set.

          that order matches the screen.

1. Tap the gear on the bottom bar, from any screen.

  Description: FRIENDLY MODE — tells it which pedal you have, so the editor speaks in names.

7.2. FRIENDLY MODE — MIDI with names
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SETTINGS › FRIENDLY MODE
Purpose: Tell it which device is on each channel so the editor shows “Drive”, “Delay” and “Reverb”
          instead of bare numbers. It's the setting that saves the most time.
How to:
1. Choose your device from the list. If you have more than one, enable per-channel mode and map each
   one.
2. From then on, every PC and CC selector in the editor shows that device's real names.
Fields and items:
- One device or several (selection)
  Description: If you use a single unit, choose it and you're done. If you have a rack with several
             devices, use per-channel mode: each MIDI channel gets a device, and names change
             according to the channel of the slot you're editing.
- CC on entering and leaving LIVE (number)
  Description: A CC fired automatically when entering LIVE mode (value 127) and when leaving (value
             0), per channel. It's for devices with “scene mode” and “stomp mode” that should follow
             the controller's screen.
- Hide unnamed PC/CC (switch)
  Description: Hides the numbers that device doesn't use from the selectors. The list keeps only
             what actually exists — and gets much shorter.
- Kemper Player (exclusive)
  Description: Choosing KEMPER PLAYER reveals an extra card with features that exist only for it:
             GET NAMES (shows the rig's name on the controller's display), FOLLOW THE KEMPER (when
             you change rig on the Kemper, the controller jumps to the matching preset) and the
             on-screen tuner, with style (arc, bar or LEDs), response speed and indicator colours.
Notes:
- Can't find your device? Choose GENERIC: everything works the same, the commands just appear by
   number.

7.3. FOOTSWITCHES — buttons outside the preset
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SETTINGS › FOOTSWITCHES
Purpose: The controls that always apply, in any preset: the six feet's operation mode, the GLOBAL
          SW, the expression pedal and the external footswitches.
Fields and items:
- Operation mode (PRESET–LIVE / HYBRID) (selection)
  Description: PRESET–LIVE is the default: in PRESET mode all feet choose presets; in LIVE, all
             control functions.
             In HYBRID, the controller splits the footswitches: some always call presets and the
             others always control effects — with no mode switching. Good if you want both at once
             and can give up half the presets per page.
- SW6 = GLOBAL SW (switch)
  Description: On models without a dedicated global button, this switch turns SW6 into a GLOBAL SW:
             it stops calling preset 6 and takes on a fixed function, the same in every preset.
             It restarts the controller when saved, because the button changes owner at boot.
- GLOBAL SW — the always-on footswitch (editor)
  Description: A footswitch outside the presets: the function set here applies in any bank, preset
             or mode. It runs the same modes as the normal footswitches (STOMP, TAP TEMPO, MACROS…).
             The classic use is the tuner, a global tap tempo or a mute — things you want to reach
             without thinking about which song you're on.
             On boards with a dedicated LIVE button, you can retire it from that job and turn it
             into a second GLOBAL SW, independent of the first.
- External expression (pedal)
  Description: Enables the expression pedal and sets the CC and channel it controls — volume, wah,
             delay mix.
             LIVE READING shows the value arriving in real time, and CALIBRATION teaches the
             controller where your pedal's minimum and maximum are (press fully each way and
             capture).
             This CC and channel are the default: a preset can have its own destination, using the
             EXP value on an extra send.
- External SW1 and SW2 (editors)
  Description: Two external footswitches connected by cable — no LED, MIDI only. Each runs the same
             modes as the others and applies in any preset.
             They're the cheap way to gain two more feet. They can also navigate banks in preview,
             leaving the six main footswitches free for effects.
Notes:
- Cards that depend on hardware (expression, external switches) only appear on models that have
   those inputs.

7.4. BANKS — power-on, navigation and combos
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SETTINGS › BANKS
Purpose: How the controller wakes up, what each footswitch does in BANK mode and which foot pairs
          fire shortcuts.
Fields and items:
- Auto Start (group)
  Description: Sets which preset the controller wakes up on when you power it — and in which mode
             (BANK or LIVE).
             With this on, the pedal comes up already calling the first song's preset, sending all
             its MIDI. You switch the power on and you're ready to play.
- Preset Call (grid)
  Description: This is where you build the controller's navigation: for each footswitch (SW1–SW6)
             and each gesture (SHORT CLICK and LONG CLICK), you choose an action. Twelve independent
             combinations.
             The main actions: CALL PRESET (calls immediately), STANDBY (opens a preview — the
             display shows the target without changing the sound, and you confirm afterwards), BANK
             UP/DOWN, the same in STANDBY form, PRESET UP/DOWN and ENTER LIVE MODE.
             At least one combination must be CALL PRESET or STANDBY — otherwise there'd be no way
             to call a preset by foot.
- STANDBY — browsing without changing the sound (action)
  Description: It deserves its own paragraph because it's the feature that changes stage life the
             most: with STANDBY you move through banks and presets while the song plays, seeing on
             the display where you're going, and the sound only changes when you confirm.
             It's like finding the next song in the middle of the previous one, with nobody hearing
             it.
- Footswitch Combos (pairs)
  Description: Pressing two footswitches together fires an action. There are nine neighbouring pairs
             — 1+2, 1+3, 2+3, 2+4, 2+5, 3+4, 3+6, 4+5 and 5+6 — each with an action of your
             choosing.
             Besides the navigation actions, combos have two exclusive ones: WI-FI ON/OFF and
             SW_LIVE (enters and exits LIVE mode).
             The action fires the moment the second foot lands, and there's no “held” version.
             Combos apply in BANK mode; the exception is SW_LIVE, which keeps working inside LIVE —
             otherwise, on boards without a dedicated button, there'd be no way back by foot.
- Active Banks (multiple selection)
  Description: Switches off the letters you don't use. Navigation then skips them, saving clicks if
             you only work with two or three banks.
             At least one letter must stay on.
Notes:
- A footswitch taking part in an active combo gets a slightly slower long click — that's the slack
   the controller gives you to form the pair with both feet.

7.5. DISPLAY — layouts, names and BPM
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SETTINGS › DISPLAY
Purpose: The display's default appearance: how many tiles appear, what gets written and what happens
          when you tap the tempo.
Fields and items:
- Gig View (selection)
  Description: Chooses which screens the controller shows on stage: DEFAULT (both), PRESET ONLY or
             LIVE ONLY. Use it to simplify the display when you only use one of the modes.
- LIVE mode and PRESET mode layouts (layout)
  Description: The default arrangement of both screens. Layouts 1 to 4 are fixed (they change how
             many tiles fit and their size), LIST shows presets in sequence and CUSTOM lets you drag
             each icon and set its size.
             PRESET also has None, the classic screen with just the big name.
             These are the global values: a preset can have its own layout, and in that case it
             wins.
- Icon shape (selection)
  Description: The tiles' shape: DEFAULT, CIRCLE or OCTAGON. Purely aesthetic, and set separately
             for each screen.
- Show the bank's preset names (switch)
  Description: On the PRESET screen, shows the six presets' names instead of the icons. It's the
             choice for those who navigate by song name rather than by icon.
- BPM on the display (group)
  Description: When you tap the tempo, the controller shows the timing in a temporary card. Here you
             choose how long it stays (OFF, 2 s, 5 s or 10 s) and whether the number is the absolute
             one (the last two taps) or the sequence's average.
             Setting OFF only hides the card — the tempo still applies for whatever uses it, like
             STEPS mode.

7.6. LEDS — ring brightness and colours
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SETTINGS › LEDS
Purpose: The LED rings around each footswitch are what you read out of the corner of your eye on
          stage. Here you set brightness and what the colours mean.
Fields and items:
- LED brightness (range)
  Description: Applies to all rings. A dark stage calls for low brightness (it avoids dazzling);
             daylight calls for high. It's also what most affects power draw.
- Banks & Presets — by letter or by switch (selection)
  Description: Decides what the colour means in BANK mode. BY LETTER: each bank has its colour, so
             the colour tells you which bank you're on. BY SWITCH: each footswitch has its own, so
             the colour tells you which preset is which.
             Choose according to what you most need to see fast.
- LED preview in LIVE (switch)
  Description: Leaves off footswitches with a faint glow instead of dark. That way you can see where
             the effects are even before switching them on — handy in the dark.
             That preview's brightness is adjusted separately, since the idea is to stay discreet
             and not compete with the actually-on effect.
- Dedicated LEDs (colours)
  Description: The colours that indicate LIVE MODE and LAYER 2. Since those two states change what
             every other foot does, it's worth picking clearly distinct colours — they're the
             warning that you're in another context.

7.7. IMAGES — your backgrounds and icons
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SETTINGS › IMAGES
Purpose: Upload your own background images and icons to the controller's display.
How to:
1. Tap an empty slot to pick a file from your device.
2. Adjust framing and brightness in the editor that opens, and save to the slot.
3. Then just choose that image in SCREEN COLOURS (background) or the icon in a footswitch's display.
Fields and items:
- Backgrounds and icons (slots)
  Description: There are two collections: backgrounds (the large, full-screen images) and icons (the
             small footswitch drawings). Each has a fixed number of slots, and the card shows how
             many are in use.
             How many slots exist depends on the model — newer boards have considerably more room.
- The image editor (tool)
  Description: Before saving you crop, zoom, adjust brightness and contrast and can even write text
             over it, with font, colour, size and outline.
             It's worth darkening backgrounds a little: on stage, an over-bright image eats the
             preset name's readability.
- Batch upload (action)
  Description: You can send several images at once and let the controller distribute them across
             free slots. There's also multiple selection for deleting several.
             Images are large files: over the USB cable the transfer is far faster and more stable
             than over Wi-Fi.

7.8. HARDWARE — model and memory
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SETTINGS › HARDWARE
Purpose: Tell the controller which board it is, and keep an eye on how much memory your presets and
          images take.
Fields and items:
- Model (family and variant) (selection)
  Description: First the family (BFMIDI 1, 2 or 3), then the variant — NANO, MICRO, 6SW+, 7S and so
             on. Each variant has its own footswitch count and layout.
             This isn't decoration: it's what tells the controller which pins exist. Choosing wrong

             written on your board.
             The editor only offers models compatible with the processor of the connected pedal, so
             you can't pick a board from another line.
- Storage (status)
  Description: How much internal memory is in use, split by presets, images, icons and global
             settings, each with used, free and total.
             Images and icons are what fills it up: if a bar is near the end, delete what you don't
             use before trying to upload more.
Notes:
- Changing the model restarts the controller — the pins are reconfigured at boot.

7.9. WIFI — network and connection
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SETTINGS › WIFI
Purpose: See how you're connected right now and, if you like, put the controller on your home
          network.
How to:
1. Tap SCAN, choose the network, type the password and CONNECT.
Fields and items:
- Connection state (status)
  Description: A card with the four possible scenarios and yours highlighted: no Wi-Fi, on the
             pedal's network (AP), on your router (STA) and over USB cable.
             When it's on the router, it shows the network name and the address to reach it from any

- SCAN and CONNECT (actions)
  Description: SCAN is only available when you're in AP mode or over cable — in STA it would drop

  Description: With the controller on your router, your phone doesn't need to switch networks to

Notes:

7.10. HOST — USB Host and Bluetooth
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SETTINGS › HOST
Purpose: The module that lets you plug USB devices straight into the controller — and Bluetooth,
          which does the same wirelessly.
Fields and items:
- USB Host status (status)
  Description: Shows the manufacturer and product of what's plugged in, plus the module's health. If
             it reads “Waiting”, nothing has been recognised yet.
- Mode (USB HOST / TONEX ONE) (selection)
  Description: Choose according to the connected device. TONEX ONE mode exists because that pedal
             speaks its own protocol; for everything else, use USB HOST.
- Translate Boss MS-3 (switch)
  Description: The MS-3 doesn't understand parameter CC/PC: the host converts to Boss's own format.
             Turn it on only with an MS-3 (or Katana) plugged in — with it on, CC 20 to 32 stop
             passing through raw.
- MIDI filter (selection)
  Description: OMNI lets every channel through; choosing a channel, only that one passes. The filter
             applies in both directions — from the controller to the USB device and back.
- BFMiDi Bluetooth (group)
  Description: When on, Bluetooth has three functions — and what separates them is who searches for
             whom:
             KEYBOARD — the controller becomes a Bluetooth keyboard; CCs turn into keys and
             shortcuts, to drive apps that don't speak MIDI (sheet music, recorder, slides).
             MIDI — it becomes a wireless MIDI interface named BFMiDi and waits: use it when the
             other device is the one searching (phone, tablet, computer).
             PEDAL — the controller searches for a blinking Bluetooth MIDI pedal and connects to it,
             to gain wireless footswitches.
Notes:
- This screen only exists on models that carry the USB Host module — Bluetooth lives in it.

7.11. EDITOR — app preferences
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SETTINGS › EDITOR
Purpose: Settings for this screen, not the controller: interface language and whether changes save
          themselves.
Fields and items:
- Auto-save (switch)
  Description: On, the editor waits a moment after each change and saves by itself, without you
             touching SAVE.
             Off (the default), nothing is stored until you say so — and leaving the preset discards
             the change. People who experiment a lot tend to prefer it off; people who forget to
             save, on.
- Interface language (selection)
  Description: Portuguese, English or Spanish. It changes the editor's labels and each card's
             explanatory text. The controller's display stays in English.
Notes:
- These preferences live on the device where the editor is open, not on the controller: opening it
   on another phone may show another language.

7.12. UPDATE — firmware and editor
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SETTINGS › UPDATE
Purpose: Keep the controller up to date. The screen checks by itself when opened and, if there's
          something new, one button handles everything.
How to:
1. Open SETTINGS › UPDATE. The check starts by itself.
2. If there's a new version, follow what the screen asks and don't unplug the cable during flashing.
Fields and items:
- The two pieces (concept)
  Description: Two things are updated together: the firmware (the program running inside the
             controller, which makes everything work) and the editor (this screen). They travel as a
             pair because a new editor may depend on features that only exist in the new firmware.
- Check status (status)
  Description: BFMIDI UP TO DATE — you're on the latest. UPDATE AVAILABLE — a new one is out. NOT
             CHECKED — the check couldn't run.
             The one downloading is your browser, not the controller: that's why the pedal doesn't
             need internet, only the device where the editor is open. Connected solely to the
             pedal's own network, “not checked” is expected.
- By cable or over the network (path)
  Description: Over the USB cable works on any model and is the rescue path — but it replaces all
             content: presets, images and icons return to the factory package.
             Over your network (on models that support it, and only with the pedal on your router)
             your presets are untouched.
             Either way, there's one safe rule: back up first.
Notes:
- Firmware and editor show the same version. If they differ, the installation was left half-done —
   run the update again.

7.13. BACKUP — copy and restore
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SETTINGS › BACKUP
Purpose: Save your work to a file — the whole pedal, a bank or a preset — and bring it back when
          needed.
How to:
1. Choose the scope, tap MAKE COPY and keep the file.
2. To bring it back, choose the file and tap RESTORE.
Fields and items:
- Full backup (file)
  Description: All the pedal's presets. You choose whether to include images and icons (it makes the
             file about ten times larger, but it's the real copy) and whether to carry the global
             settings (board, colours, Wi-Fi, controls).
             This is the backup to take before updating, before lending the pedal and every now and
             then.
- Export performance (one bank) (file)
  Description: Exports or imports a whole bank — the six presets, a gig's set — carrying only the
             images and icons that bank uses.
             On import you choose the target bank, and it is overwritten: presets missing from the
             file return to default. This is the right scope for swapping a set with someone else.
- Restoring the global settings (detail)
  Description: When the file carries the global settings, they're only rewritten if they differ from
             the current ones. In other words: you do not need to restore them after a firmware
             update — only if you erased them.
Notes:
- Keep the file off the phone (cloud, computer). A backup that lives only on the device that might
   break isn't a backup.

7.14. TESTS — diagnostics and MIDI monitor
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Related screen/preview: SETTINGS › TESTS
Purpose: Find out whether the problem is the cable, the channel or the command — without building
          any preset.
Fields and items:
- Hardware test (diagnostics)
  Description: Three ten-second tests: LEDS, DISPLAY and MIDI. Each runs inside the controller
             without freezing the rest, and the STOP button aborts.
             They answer “is it a fault or a setting?”: if the LEDs light up in the test, the
             problem is in the configuration, not the hardware.
- MIDI trigger (manual test) (tool)
  Description: Sends a MIDI message on the spot, with no preset and without entering LIVE: choose
             the channel, adjust the number with the buttons and listen.
             It's the right tool to discover which command your processor responds to — you sweep
             the numbers until the effect reacts, and only then build the preset.
             On PC, above 127 the controller sends the Bank Select along, just like a preset send.
- MIDI monitor (diagnostics)
  Description: Shows, in real time, everything the controller is sending — with time, source preset
             and the message. You can copy the list.
             It's how you check whether a preset really sends what you think it sends, and in what
             order.
Notes:
- If the manual trigger works and the preset doesn't, the problem is in the preset (channel or
   value) — not the cable or the device.

7.15. RESTORE — back to factory state
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Purpose: Erase the controller's content and start over. It's the menu's only red item, and for good
          reason.
How to:
1. Take a full backup first. There's no undo afterwards.
Fields and items:
- When it makes sense (use)
  Description: When you're passing the pedal on, when you want to restart your organisation from
             scratch, or when some setting got so tangled that starting over is faster than hunting
             the problem.
             It isn't needed before updating — an update doesn't call for scorched earth.
Notes:
- After restoring, the controller comes back with the factory presets and no combo configured.

TOPIC 8 - ADVANCED FEATURES
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Page: 5
Summary: Commands the controller sends to itself, integrations with specific devices, and what to do
        when something doesn't respond.
Introduction: Nothing on this page is required to play. These are features for those who already
        have the basics down and want to squeeze more out of the controller — or need to solve a
        specific problem.

8.1. Navigation commands (the controller talking to itself)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Purpose: Besides sending MIDI outwards, a footswitch can send a command to the controller itself —
          change bank, preset or layer without leaving LIVE mode.
How to:
1. On a footswitch in SINGLE mode, open the command list and scroll to the end: navigation commands
   come after the normal CCs, marked with ».
Fields and items:
- » UP BANK and » DOWN BANK (command)
  Description: Move one letter up or down, calling the new bank's preset. Letters switched off in
             Active Banks are skipped.
- » UP PRESET and » DOWN PRESET (command)
  Description: Step from one preset to the next inside the bank, cycling. This is how you build a
             “next song” by foot, without leaving LIVE.
- » OUT LIVE and » SW_LIVE (command)
  Description: OUT LIVE leaves LIVE mode and returns to preset navigation. SW_LIVE is the toggle: it
             enters and exits.
             On boards without a dedicated LIVE button, putting one of these on a footswitch is what
             guarantees a way back.
- » TO LAYER (command)
  Description: Switches between the preset's Layer 1 and Layer 2 — the two pages of functions. It
             only makes sense inside LIVE mode, and only if Layer 2 is enabled on that preset.
- Preview navigation (external switches) (command)
  Description: The external footswitches have two exclusive commands: bank up and down in preview,
             without changing the sound, waiting for a preset footswitch to confirm.
             It's the way to gain preview navigation without spending any of the six main feet.
Notes:
- These commands don't go out the MIDI cable: they're consumed by the controller itself.

8.2. Integrations — Kemper and Valeton GP-5
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Purpose: Two devices have their own conversations with the controller, going beyond ordinary MIDI.
Fields and items:
- Kemper Player (integration)
  Description: Choosing KEMPER PLAYER in Friendly Mode gives the controller: the rig name on the
             display, the tuner on screen (with adjustable style and colours) and FOLLOW THE KEMPER
             — when you change rig on the device, the controller jumps by itself to the matching
             preset.
             On top of that, the command list starts including the Kemper's internal parameters by
             name, not just generic CCs.
- Valeton GP-5 (integration)
  Description: Connected over USB, the GP-5 accepts things that don't exist as ordinary CCs.
             Choosing it in Friendly Mode gives you its internal parameters in the command list —
             the same as turning its knobs from the controller.
             And TAP TEMPO starts sending the delay time in milliseconds instead of simulating taps:
             the delay gets the tempo right the first time.
Notes:
- Both integrations require the device plugged into the controller's USB Host (or the controller
   into its USB, in the Kemper's case).

8.3. When something doesn't respond
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Purpose: A short routine to find the problem in the right order, from most likely to rarest.
Fields and items:
- The effects unit doesn't change sound (routine)
  Description: 1. Does the cable run from the BFMiDi to the unit (MIDI OUT → MIDI IN)?
             2. Does the preset's channel match what the unit listens to?
             3. Did you tap SAVE?
             4. Use MIDI TRIGGER in SETTINGS › TESTS: if it works there, the problem is in the
             preset; if not even there, it's the cable or the device.
- I can't open the editor (routine)
  Description: The controller's Wi-Fi turns itself off after 90 seconds with nobody connected. Power
             the controller off and on: Wi-Fi comes up at boot and you get 90 seconds to join.
             If it happens often, set a combo to the WI-FI ON/OFF action, or put the controller on
             your home network — that way it's always reachable.
- A footswitch does nothing (routine)
  Description: Check that it isn't set to MUTE, and that you're in the right mode (PRESET × LIVE) —
             in LIVE it does what the preset assigned, it doesn't change presets.
             Also check the board model in SETTINGS › HARDWARE: the wrong model leaves footswitches
             without function.
- I can't upload images (routine)
  Description: Check storage in SETTINGS › HARDWARE: if the images bar is full, delete what you
             don't use first.
             And prefer the USB cable for media — they're large files, and over Wi-Fi the transfer
             is slower and more prone to failing.
Notes:
- Before touching anything big, take a backup. It's what turns a mistake into five lost minutes
   rather than a lost night.
